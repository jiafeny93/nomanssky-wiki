You wake up next to a wrecked starship on a planet that is actively trying to kill you. That has been the opening minute of No Man's Sky since 2016, and in 2026 it is greeting a bigger crowd than the game has seen in years: the tenth anniversary landed on August 9, Hello Games announced the free Cosmos update in the same breath, and returning veterans and brand-new travelers are landing side by side. Starting fresh in the middle of all that is genuinely a good time to start — the game has never been deeper — but the first hours are exactly where new players flounder, because the tutorial explains the buttons and not the plan.

This page is the plan: a ten-hour roadmap from crash site to a fully spacefaring save — fix your ship, claim a base, earn your first million units, get a starship worth the name, learn to warp, and take a freighter off pirate hands for free. Each stage ends where our deep-dive guides begin, so you can drop out of the roadmap and go deep whenever a system clicks.

The short version:

1. **Hour one:** follow the Awakenings quest and repair only what it asks for — launch thrusters, pulse engine, enough hazard protection to survive the walk between them.
2. **Hours one to three:** place a Base Computer somewhere you like, learn the Analysis Visor, and start collecting free exosuit slots from every space station you dock at.
3. **Hours three to six:** turn scanning and surface finds into your first few million units, then spend them on a ship worth flying instead of the wreck you woke up next to.
4. **Hours six to ten:** stockpile warp cells, get introduced to the Space Anomaly, and rescue your first freighter from a pirate ambush — for free.
5. **After that:** expeditions, freighter fleets, exotic ships, and the new Cosmos features. The final section of this guide maps all of it.

## Hour One: Crash Site to Liftoff

The opening mission, Awakenings, is a good tutorial wearing an apocalypse's clothes. Follow it. It walks you through charging your hazard protection, mining your first resources, and repairing the wreck, and it pays out blueprints you will use for the rest of the game. The biggest first-hour mistake is treating your starter planet as home — it is not, and you are not meant to stay.

Gather these five things before you worry about anything else:

| Resource | Where it comes from | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium | Yellow flowers and crystals | Recharges hazard protection |
| Oxygen | Red flowers and plants | Recharges life support |
| Carbon | Trees, shrubs, and small fauna | Fuels the mining beam |
| Ferrite Dust | Rocks and large deposits | Becomes Pure Ferrite and Metal Plating |
| Di-Hydrogen | Blue crystal clusters | Becomes launch fuel and Di-Hydrogen Jelly |

**The repairs that matter** are the launch thrusters and the pulse engine. The thrusters want Pure Ferrite and a Di-Hydrogen Jelly; the pulse engine wants a Metal Plating and a Hermetic Seal. Fifty Ferrite Dust becomes the Pure Ferrite, another fifty becomes the plating, and forty Di-Hydrogen becomes the jelly — the on-screen recipe hints do the math for you, so this is mostly a walking tour of your crash site's surroundings. The Hermetic Seal is the one part you cannot craft yet: the quest marks a point of interest nearby where one is waiting, and the trip there doubles as your first real walk across a planet — and possibly your first meeting with the Sentinels, the robotic police you will be dodging for the next hundred hours.

Everything else on the wreck — deflector shields, photon cannon — can stay broken until later. Essentials only. Craft launch fuel before you take off (or feed the thrusters uranium if the planet happens to have it), climb, and leave the atmosphere. The moment you can pulse-drive across space, Awakenings keeps handing you markers: follow them. The quest chain is the tutorial, and cutting it short is how new players end up broke, blueprintless, and stuck.

## Hours One to Three: First Base, Analysis Visor, Free Suit Slots

Shortly after liftoff the quest log hands you a **Base Computer**. Plant it wherever you want to live, with three practical criteria: weather you can stand, terrain you can build on, and a view you will not get tired of, because you can teleport back here from any space station for the rest of the game. Your first base does not need to be ambitious — four walls, a door, and a save point — and the Base Computer mission line unlocks the whole construction research system as you go.

The currency of that system is **Salvaged Data**, and it is buried literally everywhere. Use the Analysis Visor on any planet surface and you will see Buried Technology sites marked in scanning vision; dig them up with the Terrain Manipulator (the early quests hand it to you) and each one yields Salvaged Data. Spend it at the Construction Research Unit inside your own base to unlock building parts — rooms, power, storage, farming. Two or three sites fund everything a first base needs.

The **Analysis Visor** is also your first income stream, and most new players underuse it for their whole first day. Scan every new creature, plant, and mineral you see: each first-time discovery pays units, and uploading your discoveries pays a little more. The multiplier comes from scanner upgrades — buy Scanner Modules from the multi-tool technology merchant on any space station and slot them in. A stack of good ones turns single scans into six-figure payouts, which quietly funds your entire early game while you walk around doing what you were doing anyway.

Three more systems open up in this window:

- **Vocabulary.** Talk to every alien you meet and activate every Knowledge Stone you pass. Words unlock one at a time, and understanding what NPCs are saying turns random interactions into free loot and standing with the game's three factions.
- **Exosuit inventory.** Every space station sells exactly one exosuit slot expansion, and the price only climbs with how many you own — so buy the one on every station you visit. Drop pods on planets and the terminal on the Space Anomaly add more. Inventory is the true early-game bottleneck; slots are the upgrade you feel immediately.
- **The refiner.** The portable refiner the quest line gives you turns raw materials into better materials — Ferrite Dust into Pure Ferrite, for a start — and it is the doorway to the entire crafting economy later on.

If money is the thing you want more of, the next section is the funnel; our full [money-making guide](/guides/money-making-guide) ranks every method in the game by effort and payout, from your first hour to fully passive endgame farms.

## Hours Three to Six: Your First Million and a Real Starship

Units are the game's first wall: everything you want — ships, suit slots, freighter tech — is priced in units, and the story quests pay pocket change. Three methods carry new players over the wall, and which one is best depends on what your corner of the galaxy looks like:

| Method | Where it works | What it pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery scanning | Everywhere, from minute one | Six figures per new find once scanner modules are stacked |
| Ancient bones | Planets surveyed as containing ancient bones | Rare specimens sell for around 1.5 million units each |
| Storm crystals | Extreme-weather planets, during active storms | Over 200,000 units per crystal, 20-plus per good storm |

Scanning is the zero-risk baseline you are already doing. Ancient bones turn arid fossil planets into digging grounds — bring the Terrain Manipulator and empty cargo slots. Storm crystals only surface while a storm is raging, so they pair with hazard protection upgrades and an exit plan. All three scale with the same two things: exosuit slots (which you are buying on every station) and a ship with cargo space, which is the second half of this stage.

**Do not buy your first ship at full price.** The wreck-repair economy is the beginner's best friend, and there are three smart first upgrades:

| Option | Cost | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A crashed ship | Repair materials only | Find wrecks with distress-signal charts, repair the essentials, fly it or scrap it for units and parts |
| A shuttle | A few million units | Cheap, balanced, no fatal weakness — the [ship tier list](/starships/best-ships) calls it the sensible starter for good reason |
| A Sentinel interceptor | Free | Top-tier stats claimed, not bought, from dissonant planets — combat-gated instead of credit-gated |

The interceptor deserves the spotlight: it is arguably the best early power spike in the game, because the price is a fight rather than a fortune. The full loop — finding dissonant systems, destroying Dissonant Resonators, claiming the wreck — is in our [sentinel interceptor guide](/starships/sentinel-interceptor-guide). And once units stop being scary, shop the way veterans do: wealthy three-star economies, where [S-class spawn odds](/starships/s-class-ships) roughly double to about two percent. If launch fuel is the tax annoying you most, a [solar ship](/starships/solar-ship-guide) eventually deletes it from your life entirely — that is a mid-game purchase, but worth knowing the exit exists.

Two smaller currencies start mattering around now. **Nanites** buy upgrade modules and blueprints — station mission boards and scrapping unwanted ships are the entry-level sources, and the efficient loops are in our [nanites farming guide](/guides/nanites-farming). **Quicksilver** buys the special cosmetics and the Void Egg that grows a living ship, and it comes almost exclusively from group missions at the Nexus — our [quicksilver farming guide](/guides/quicksilver-farming) covers the weekly rhythm.

## Hours Six to Ten: Warp Cells, the Anomaly, and a Free Freighter

Your starter system is a pond; the galaxy is the point. The story walks you through the hyperdrive, the antimatter recipe, and warp cell crafting — build a small stockpile and keep it, because early quests and later expeditions both assume you can jump on demand. The moment warps feel routine, two things happen that change the shape of the game.

First, **Nada and Polo hail you** and you are introduced to the Space Anomaly, the traveling station you can summon from anywhere once the story introduces it. The Anomaly is the game's hub: technology vendors, the Quicksilver Synthesis Companion, the Nexus with its co-op missions, and — quietly, in a corner — the Expedition Terminal. It is also where you make the hyperdrive worth having: the module-stacking rules and range math that turn a starter drive into a galaxy-crosser are covered in our [hyperdrive upgrades guide](/starships/hyperdrive-upgrades).

Second, **you start getting into fights you did not start.** After roughly three hours of play and five warps since your last rescue, warping into a system can drop you into a pirate ambush on a capital freighter. Win it. The captain hands you the keys to the freighter — free, no strings. This is the single biggest freebie in the game and it is timed to arrive exactly when a new player needs a mobile base of operations.

The fine print is one decision: the first free offer is always a standard freighter, while capital-class hulls — the big war-veteran ships with 34-plus fleet slots — only ever come from later rescue battles, and from the third rescue onward they are always capitals. Taking the free standard freighter immediately is a completely fine choice; your future self might prefer you decline and let the cycle run toward a capital. Either way, running a freighter (bases, frigate expeditions, passive income) is its own game — the [freighter guide](/starships/freighter-guide) covers living in one, and the [capital freighter farming guide](/starships/capital-freighter-farming) covers landing an S-class capital deliberately.

This is also when **expeditions** enter the picture: seasonal runs on their own save file, with a fixed milestone list and exclusive ship and armor rewards. As of August 18, 2026, no expedition is live — Expedition 22, The Swarm, closed on July 19 — but past expeditions can be replayed from the Anomaly terminal any time, and the tenth-anniversary update makes a new run likely. Learn the two ways to start one in our [expedition starter guide](/expeditions/how-to-start-expedition); the system-level deep dive is the [main expedition guide](/expeditions/expedition-guide), and our [Expedition 23 tracker](/expeditions/expedition-23) will carry the announcement the moment it lands. In a hurry when it does? The [fast completion guide](/expeditions/expedition-fast-completion) explains phase thresholds and what you can skip.

## Ten Beginner Mistakes That Cost Hours

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Do this instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colonizing the starter planet | Your first system is random and often awful | Treat it as a tutorial zone; everything within a few warps can be better |
| Repairing the whole starter ship | Shields and cannon burn materials you need for warps | Repair the thrusters and pulse engine; nothing else |
| Never using the Analysis Visor | First-scan units and uploaded discoveries walk past you | Scan every new lifeform, plant, and rock, everywhere, forever |
| Buying ships at full price early | Wrecks and shuttles do the same job for a fraction | Claim crashed hulls, repair essentials, scrap the rest |
| Skipping exosuit terminals on stations | Inventory is the real early-game bottleneck | One slot per station, every station, until prices hurt |
| Dumping your whole cargo in one sale | Markets crash the price as you flood them | Sell in batches, and split big hauls across systems |

## What to Play After Hour Ten

Hour ten is the end of the on-ramp, not the game — this site exists for everything after it. A few directions, depending on what pulled at you:

**The Cosmos era.** You are starting No Man's Sky in the same season its two oldest rules are being rewritten: flying directly between star systems instead of only warping, and landing on stars themselves with new heat-resistant gear. The [Cosmos new features roundup](/cosmos-update/cosmos-new-features) tracks every confirmed change, with the [interstellar flight guide](/cosmos-update/interstellar-flight-guide) comparing free flight against the classic warp workflow you just learned, and the [star landing guide](/cosmos-update/how-to-land-on-stars) covering the approach and the preparation checklist. Everything else that shipped this year is in the [2026 update tracker](/guides/new-update-2026).

**Ships worth the grind.** Exotics always spawn in S-class and are hunted by portal address — the [exotic ship locations guide](/starships/exotic-ship-locations) teaches the glyph-and-portal pipeline. Living ships are grown, not bought, through the Starbirth questline; the [living ship guide](/starships/living-ship-guide) walks the whole arc from Void Egg onward.

**The expedition circuit.** Once Expedition 23 is announced, everything you learned in the warp section compounds: the [expedition guide](/expeditions/expedition-guide) for the system, the [fast completion guide](/expeditions/expedition-fast-completion) for the routing, and the [Expedition 23 page](/expeditions/expedition-23) for the schedule.

Wherever you go next, the first ten hours did their job: you can fly, fund yourself, and pick a direction. That is the whole skill of No Man's Sky — the rest is choosing what to chase across a galaxy that, ten years in, nobody has come close to finishing.
