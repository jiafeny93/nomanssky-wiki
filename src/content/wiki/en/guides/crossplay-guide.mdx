No Man's Sky has had full crossplay since 2020, and it remains one of the quietest triumphs of the game's redemption arc: a Steam player, a PlayStation player, an Xbox player, and a Switch player can share one galaxy, one base, and one freighter with no adapters, no re-purchase, and no separate edition to buy. With the Cosmos update cycle bringing waves of returning and brand-new players in 2026, "how do I play with my friend" is once again one of the most-asked questions in the community.

**Last updated: August 18, 2026.** This guide covers how crossplay works on patch 6.50: turning it on, adding friends across platforms, joining a session, what happens to your save, and the subscription fine print.

## Does No Man's Sky Have Crossplay?

Yes — full, platform-wide crossplay between PC (Steam, GOG, Epic, and PC Game Pass), PlayStation, Xbox, and Nintendo Switch. It shipped in 2020 as a free update and has been a foundation system ever since. Everyone plays in one shared universe, meets the same travelers at the Space Anomaly, and can group up regardless of platform.

What crossplay gives you:

- **Shared multiplayer sessions** — up to a group of four traveling, building, and fighting together
- **Shared social spaces** — the Space Anomaly's Nexus is a cross-platform lobby
- **Cross-platform group expeditions** — friends on any platform can run [expeditions](/expeditions/expedition-guide) together, each on their own fresh save
- **One friend list** — an in-game No Man's Sky friends system that works across every platform, separate from your console friends list

## How to Enable Crossplay

For most players it is already on — crossplay and network play default to enabled. To check:

1. Open the **Options / Settings** menu (Pause, then Settings).
2. Go to the **Network & Voice** section.
3. Confirm **Multiplayer / network play is enabled**.
4. Check your visibility settings — you can be visible to everyone, friends only, or hidden, controlling who can see and join you.

That is the entire setup. There is no external account to link, no launcher, no crossplay pass, and nothing was ever sold to unlock it.

## How to Add Cross-Platform Friends

Because your Steam friends and your friend's PlayStation friends live in different walled gardens, No Man's Sky ships its own cross-platform friend system:

| Method | How it works | Best for |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| In-game friends menu | Add a friend directly from the multiplayer/friends menu in-game | The universal method — works between any two platforms |
| Meeting in the Anomaly | Players you meet at the Nexus can be added on the spot | Organic meetings; join a group first to make it stick |
| Session history | Recent players you've grouped with appear in your list | Re-adding someone you played a mission with |

Once someone is on your in-game friends list, their platform stops mattering — you can see them online, join them, and invite them from any device.

## How to Play With Friends, Step by Step

1. **Form the group first.** One player opens the multiplayer/group menu and invites the others from their in-game friends list. Cross-platform invites arrive in-game regardless of platform.
2. **Accept the invite, then join the session.** Accepting an invitation (or selecting "join game" on a friend) loads you into the host's session — you appear near them, in their system, almost always right beside their current planet or freighter.
3. **Sync up physically.** Joining places you in the host's system but not always at their shoulder — the host should stay put and drop a **Save Beacon or base marker** to fly toward, or share the system's **portal address** (the 16-glyph coordinate every location has) so a guest can portal straight there from any space station.
4. **Play.** Missions, base building, freighter raids, pet battles, and expedition milestones all work in a group. Progress — units, discoveries, mission credit — is earned individually on each player's own save.
5. **Leave whenever.** Quitting the session returns you to your own universe with everything you earned. Nothing about your save lives on the host's machine.

Two honest quirks to expect: session joins occasionally take a second try (a known multiplayer jank, retry the invite), and groups can briefly desync visually inside crowded bases or freighters — walk outside and it re-syncs.

## Crossplay vs Cross-Save: What Carries Over

This is where players get burned, so read this table twice:

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Can I play *with* friends on other platforms? | Yes, fully — that is crossplay |
| Does my **save** transfer between platforms? | **No** — a PlayStation save and a Steam save are separate universes |
| Exceptions? | Same platform family: PS4↔PS5 and Xbox consoles share saves via their ecosystems; PC Game Pass and Xbox share through the Microsoft ecosystem |
| Can I copy a save manually? | Not supported across platform families — there is no official save transfer |
| Do purchased items or currency cross over? | Not applicable — the game has no microtransactions, so there is nothing purchased to strand |

The practical rule: **pick the platform you will actually keep playing on**, because your hundreds of hours live there forever. If you are choosing a platform fresh, our [Switch 2 performance guide](/guides/switch-2-performance) and [2026 review](/guides/worth-playing-2026) cover the trade-offs.

## Do I Need a Subscription to Play Online?

| Platform | Online multiplayer requires |
|----------|----------------------------|
| PC (Steam / GOG / Epic) | Nothing beyond the platform itself |
| PlayStation | A PlayStation Plus membership, per Sony's standard online policy |
| Xbox | An Xbox online membership (Game Pass Core), per Microsoft's standard online policy |
| Switch | A Nintendo Switch Online membership, per Nintendo's standard online policy |

Solo play, including the full campaign and all updates, requires no subscription on any platform. The in-game friends and crossplay systems themselves are free — only each console platform's own online policy applies, as with any online console game.
