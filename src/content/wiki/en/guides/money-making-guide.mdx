Exosuit slots cost hundreds of thousands each, a good A-class ship runs eight figures, a freighter runs nine, and the answer to "can I afford it" is always the same verb: farm. The difference between the broke and the rich is not playtime — it is picking a method whose hourly rate matches the gear you have, instead of grinding the first loop you found.

## How Money Works in No Man's Sky

Three currencies circulate, and confusing them wastes hours. **Units** buy everything physical: ships, freighters, frigates, slots, resources, and trade goods. **Nanites** buy upgrade modules and blueprints — our [nanites farming guide](/guides/nanites-farming) covers that economy separately, though the two loops feed each other, since scrapping ships pays in both. **Quicksilver** buys cosmetics and special items from the Anomaly vendor, mostly earned from missions — see the [quicksilver farming guide](/guides/quicksilver-farming), including the February 2026 change that added Quicksilver drops from destroying ice.

One structural fact shapes every strategy below: the unit cap sits around **4.29 billion**, and a single trade terminal transaction tops out near **2 billion** — roughly 130 Stasis Devices. The cap sounds theoretical until your farm prints for a month straight. Players who reach it stop farming units and start farming the other two currencies, which is a reasonable life plan.

## Trade Routes: The Classic Loop

Trading is the game's intended money engine, and it still works exactly as designed. Every system has an economy type — mining, trading, manufacturing, technology, scientific, and so on — and each economy sells its own commodity goods cheaply while paying a premium for the goods other economies produce. The loop is buy low in one economy, warp or teleport to the economy that wants it, sell high, buy that system's goods, and continue until your hold is sick of it.

The numbers to watch on each station terminal are the **buy and sell percentage modifiers**. The documented sweet spots: buy goods at **-20% to -30%** below base value and sell them at **+70% to +80%** above it. Install an **Economy Scanner** on your ship so the galaxy map can filter for wealthy 3-star economies — the same module that anchors the [S-class ship hunt](/starships/s-class-ships) — because wealthy systems both stock more expensive goods and pay the best sell rates.

Two mechanics separate a 5M hour from a 50M hour. First, **selling crashes the local price**: dump a full hold of one commodity in a system and that system's price for it tanks, so you sell once and leave. Second, **teleporters beat warping**: once you have visited a set of good buy and sell systems, teleporting between their stations skips fuel, pirate interdictions, and most of the travel time. Community-tested loops that hit each station in about two minutes — sell, buy, teleport — report **roughly 50M units per half hour**; the classic warp-driven version from older guides earned about 1.7M per hour, which tells you how much the teleporter matters.

## Scrapping Free Interceptors: The Best Rate in the Game

The single fastest units-per-minute loop in 2026 costs nothing to start. On dissonant planets, fighting Sentinels and using an **Echo Locator** reveals a **Harmonic Camp**, and each camp's terminal points to a crashed **Sentinel interceptor** you can claim for free — no purchase, no repair bill worth mentioning. Fly it to a space station's Starship Outfitting Terminal and scrap it: even C-class interceptors pay solid scrap value, with the crash-site condition making them pure profit. Players report **30-50M units in a few minutes** of claim-and-scrap cycling (community-tested), plus Storage Augmentations and upgrade modules as side output.

Know the two limits. Scrap value has **diminishing returns**: repeated claims in one session have been observed paying 22M, then 18M, then 16.5M for identical ships, so the loop decays and you should rotate activities when the payouts sag. And the loop needs dissonant systems, so an Economy Scanner or a conflict-level filter helps you chain them. This is also the units-positive cousin of the buy-and-scrap loop in our [S-class ships guide](/starships/s-class-ships), where you pay tens of millions for a hull and profit in slots and modules instead of units.

## Smuggling and Piracy: The Outlaw Payday

The Outlaws update built an entire criminal economy, and it remains one of the highest-margin trades in the game. Outlaw stations — marked by the skull icon on the galaxy map — have a **Contraband Agent** selling prohibited goods at a discount. Haul that contraband to regulated space and sell it in a **wealthy 3-star economy**: documented markups run **+150% to +180%** over base value on goods like Banned Weapons, which carry an ~83,000-unit base price. The margin collapses to low single digits if you sell at a random poor station, so the sell-side economy choice matters more than the buy.

The risk side is thinner than the flavor suggests. Pirate systems can interdict you in transit, but **teleporting directly to a station terminal skips hostile cargo scans entirely**, which is why optimized smugglers route everything through teleporter networks. One community-tested run toured **60 outlaw stations in about two hours by teleporter and cleared more than 50M units** — effectively a trade loop with contraband instead of legal commodities.

Outright piracy still pays worse than smuggling but needs no capital: raiding freighter **cargo pods** in space yields trade commodities and salvage you sell at the next station, and outlaw stations also host **Bounty Masters** whose pirate missions pay units and upgrades. Treat piracy as the flavor on top of a smuggling loop rather than its replacement, and see our [best ships](/starships/best-ships) rankings for the fighters that survive the wanted heat.

## Frigate Fleets: The Passive Engine

Once you own a freighter, frigates turn money into a background process. Buy frigates from the fleets visiting systems (roughly **1.5M to 12.5M units** depending on class and type), then send squadrons of up to five on timed expeditions from the freighter bridge. Expeditions complete in real time whether you play or not, returning units, trade commodities, crafting resources, and occasionally frigate upgrade items. Individual runs commonly pay **upwards of 12M units** each (community-tested), and a large, well-upgraded fleet running multiple simultaneous expeditions brings players **50-70M units per day** almost unattended.

The catch is the ramp. A fleet big enough to fill every expedition slot costs more than most players earn in their first twenty hours, frigate stats need to be groomed — balanced stats across combat, exploration, industry, trade, and support beat one-dimensional ships — and missions occasionally fail and damage ships if you under-send. The payoff is that frigates keep earning while you are off doing anything else, including the other methods in this guide, which is why endgame players run all of them at once. Our [freighter guide](/starships/freighter-guide) covers fleet logistics, and the [capital freighter farming guide](/starships/capital-freighter-farming) covers getting the S-class capital that anchors it all.

## Stasis Device and Fusion Ignitor Farms: The Ceiling

The two most expensive craftables in the game both sell for **about 15.6 million units each**, and neither cares where you sell them — they are crafted products, not trade goods, so no economy bonus applies and no market crashes. The blueprints come from Manufacturing Facilities and Operations Centres on planets, or from the Synthesis Laboratory aboard the Space Anomaly for **250 nanites each**.

The catch is the recipe tree. A Stasis Device chains quantum processors, cryogenic chambers, and secret ingredients; a Fusion Ignitor chains thermal components and enriched carbon — and underneath all of it sit gas harvesters pulling radon, nitrogen, and sulphurine, plus planetary farms growing crops like frostwort, solanium, and cactus flesh. A complete self-sufficient base that turns raw gas and sunlight into 15.6M-unit items is weeks of building, and that is the point: it is the game's retirement plan. Established farms report harvests of ten-plus devices per cycle — **150M+ units per run** (community-tested) — repeating forever with only refiner time in between. Build toward this once, and units stop being a constraint for the rest of your save.

## Early-Game Money Before You Have Anything

Hours zero through five have their own ladder, and it starts with the **Analysis Visor**. Stack S-class scanner upgrade modules and each fauna scan pays out dramatically — documented module bonuses reach **+8,000% to +10,000%**, with community-tested X-class stacks pushing +30,000%, which works out to **130,000-150,000 units for a common fauna scan** and 1.5 times that for uncommon ones. A walk across an unscanned planet with a stacked visor is legitimately a multi-million-unit activity, and it upgrades itself: scan money buys the cargo slots that make every other method faster.

On planets, three pickup loops fill the gap. **Ancient bones and salvageable scrap** dig sites pay roughly **1-2M units per haul** (community-tested) for nothing but a terrain manipulator and patience. **Storm crystals**, which only spawn during storms on extreme-weather planets, sell for around **100,000+ units each** in community reports — about triple a **Gravitino Ball** (~40,000 base value), and collecting them does not summon sentinels the way Gravitino Balls do. And whispering eggs around abandoned buildings yield larval cores worth quick tens of thousands early on. None of these compete with the big loops; all of them fund the first freighter warp.

## The Nerf Graveyard: Old Metas, Honestly Assessed

Every long-running game's economy has ghosts, and pretending otherwise would make this guide useless in six months. Three deserve specific honesty.

**Activated Indium farming was the meta, and it is dead at scale.** Before the Waypoint update, Activated Indium sold for about 949 units each — the most expensive element in the game — and single farms printed hundreds of millions passively. The nerf cut the price roughly **sixfold to around 165 units**, and extraction rates now suffer diminishing returns. Large extractor stacks still pull thousands per hour (one community setup reports 9,200 per hour), so the farm still trickles millions daily; it is just no longer the endgame answer, and new players should not spend their first week building one.

**The buy-oxygen chlorine loop is gone; the farm-oxygen version survives.** Chlorine expansion — 1 chlorine plus 2 oxygen refining into 6 — once paired with oxygen bought cheaply from trade terminals into an infinite money printer. Hello Games removed oxygen from most terminals and trimmed chlorine's value, killing the purchased-oxygen version. With your own oxygen hotspot and refiner bank, community players still report **10-50M units per hour** of semi-passive output. Alive, nerfed, and no longer lazy.

**Market crashes are not a nerf but a rule.** Sell enough of anything in one system and the price collapses — this is why every trading method above says "sell once, leave." Loops that ignore it look broken and are not.

## Expedition Rewards Are Not a Units Farm

Expeditions deserve a slot on this list mostly to correct expectations. Their milestone rewards are overwhelmingly cosmetics and account-wide unlocks — redeemed free on every save afterward through the Quicksilver Synthesis Companion on the Anomaly — and the reward tracks occasionally include items you can sell, typically in the low hundreds of thousands of units. That is a rounding error next to an hour of scrapping interceptors. Run expeditions for the exclusive gear, using our [fast completion guide](/expeditions/expedition-fast-completion), and check the [expedition rewards list](/expeditions/expedition-rewards-list) for what is worth claiming — not for units.

## Which Method Should You Actually Run?

Match the loop to your save's stage:

| Career stage | Run this | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hour 0-5 | Stacked scanner modules, bones, storm crystals | Zero capital, funds first suit slots |
| Hour 5-20 | Interceptor scrapping, first trade routes | 30M+ hours with no fleet needed |
| Hour 20+ | Add smuggling loops, start buying frigates | Converts active income into passive |
| Established | Stasis Device farm over a maxed frigate fleet | The permanent 4-billion-unit machine |

The honest meta-answer: the richest players do not pick one. They keep a frigate fleet out at all times, a craft farm maturing in the background, and scrapping or trade loops for any session where they actively need units in the next hour.

## Does the Cosmos Update Change the Economy?

Not yet, and possibly not directly. As of August 18, 2026, Hello Games is still releasing [Cosmos](/cosmos-update/cosmos-update-guide) patch notes in stages, and nothing published so far touches prices, trade rates, farming yields, or frigate returns. The tables and rates above reflect the current live game.

The plausible interaction is new supply. Confirmed star landings with heat-resistant gear imply new places to go and, historically, new places to go eventually mean new resources and tradeables. That is speculation, not patch notes — but it is why our [2026 update tracker](/guides/new-update-2026) recommends banking units before the full rollout lands: new technology has always cost currency, and Cosmos is shaping up to add some.
