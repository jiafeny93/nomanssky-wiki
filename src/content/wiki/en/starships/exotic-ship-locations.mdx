Exotic ships are the rarest hull class you can buy in No Man's Sky: angular squids, shield-gilled guppies, and ribbed sails that no other ship in the game looks like. They **always spawn in S-class**, they roll strong stats across the board, and every system that has one has exactly one exotic design — which is why the community trades locations so aggressively. Most of that trading happens through portal coordinates, the 12-glyph addresses that let any player dial directly into another player's find.

The catch in 2026 is access. The big community coordinate exchanges have been unreliable lately, and a shared address on a forum is only as good as the day it was posted. So this guide is deliberately method-first: how exotic spawning actually works, how to find one yourself with the trading post loop, how to dial anyone's glyph address through a portal, and how to share your own finds so they actually help people. **Last updated: August 17, 2026.**

## What Makes an Exotic Ship Special

Exotics occupy a strange niche. They are not a separate stat archetype the way fighters or haulers are — they are a rarity tier expressed as a ship. Three things define them:

| Trait | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Class | **Always S-class** — no rolling for a better tier |
| Stats | High and balanced: strong damage, shields, and hyperdrive together |
| Inventory | Smaller than a dedicated hauler or big fighter — you trade slots for prestige and stats |
| Spawn rate | Rare in any system, regardless of economy wealth |
| Design | One fixed exotic model per system; no two neighboring systems share one |

That last row is the important one for coordinates. Because a system's exotic is deterministic, an address posted today still points at the same ship next year. Coordinates don't expire the way stat rolls or loot tables might — they just get harder to verify when nobody is maintaining the databases anymore.

The practical appeal is simple: an exotic is the only ship where the S-class stat ceiling is guaranteed the moment it lands. For everything else about whether an exotic fits your build, see our ship class breakdown in [the best ships ranked](/starships/best-ships).

## How Exotic Spawning Actually Works

Ships in No Man's Sky arrive at trading outposts and space stations in waves. When you load into a location, the game seeds a queue of ships that land over the following minutes; reloading a save re-rolls that queue. Exotics compete for slots in those waves like any other ship, just at very low weight.

Two community observations, held consistently for years, drive every farming method:

1. **Exotics can appear in any system** — wealthy, average, or poor economy alike. Economy tier gates S-class odds for normal ships, not exotic spawns.
2. **Wealthy economies are still where you hunt**, because if the exotic doesn't show, a **3-star economy** (Opulent, Affluent, Prosperous) gives normal ships their best S-class odds — roughly **2%** per spawn instead of about 1% in average economies and near zero in poor ones. A failed exotic hunt in a rich system still pays out in S-class fighters and explorers.

The first ships to land after a load — the "first wave" — is where most hunters focus, because it is the fastest thing to re-roll.

## Finding an Exotic Yourself: The Trading Post Loop

This is the self-reliant method. It needs no glyphs, no portals, and no coordinates from anyone:

1. Install an **Economy Scanner** on your starship, then use the galaxy map to filter for **wealthy economies** (3-star: Opulent, Affluent, Prosperous).
2. In a wealthy system, pulse toward a planet and land at a **trading outpost** — the Economy Scanner's outpost scan or planetary charts from a station cartographer will mark one.
3. Step out of your ship. Exiting your ship creates an autosave — this is your reset point.
4. Watch the ships that land. Target each one and scan it to read class, stats, and inventory before you commit to anything.
5. If nothing exotic appears within the first couple of waves, open the options menu and **reload your restore point**. The wave queue re-rolls.
6. Repeat. Reloads are fast, and each cycle re-rolls both the first wave and the S-class lottery for every other ship in it.

Bring patience and units. Hunters routinely report dozens of reloads for a single exotic, and even the failed loops in a rich system surface S-class hardware worth buying. When your exotic finally lands, walk up, check the price — expect tens of millions of units — and buy it before it departs.

Space stations work with the same loop: dock, exit your ship inside the station to autosave, watch the landing pads, and reload if the waves disappoint. Outposts are usually preferred simply because waves arrive faster and the camera angle is better.

## Using Portals: Dialing Someone Else's Find

Portal addresses are how a stranger's exotic becomes yours. The system has three parts: learning your glyphs, finding and charging a portal, and dialing the address.

### Learn Your 16 Glyphs

1. Fly to any space station and look for a **Traveler NPC** — the floating, hooded figures. They spawn randomly, so check several stations.
2. Speak to the Traveler and pay **100 nanites**. It marks a grave on a planet in that system.
3. Land at the grave and interact with it. You learn **one glyph** per grave.
4. Repeat in new systems until all **16 glyphs** are known. Plan on 16 systems and a couple of hours.

There is no shortcut and no ordering requirement — glyph 7 is as easy to learn as glyph 1, you just need 16 graves total.

### Find and Charge a Portal

Portals are large alien structures on planets, one per world at most. Two reliable ways to locate one:

1. Buy **planetary charts** from the cartographer on any space station — the charts that search for alien structures and monoliths are the type that can mark a portal. Use them from orbit or on the surface and follow the icon.
2. Alternatively, find a **Monolith** (same charts) and interact with it. With decent standing in that system's dominant race, the Monolith offers a locate-portal option that pings the portal directly.

At the portal, activate it and dial: selecting each glyph slot asks for a small amount of a resource to charge it. The portal names the material per slot — common elements cover most of them, so a mixed stack of basics is enough.

### Dial the Address and Go

1. Enter the **12 glyphs** of the address exactly as posted, left to right.
2. Confirm, and the portal fires you — with your ship — to that address's portal, anywhere in the galaxy.
3. To get home afterward, summon the **Space Anomaly** or teleport from any space station you have visited; you do not need to fly back the long way.

### What to Do When You Arrive

The address only takes you to the portal's planet. The ship is where the poster says it is:

1. If the exotic was found at the **space station**, pulse up, dock, and work the landing pads with the same first-wave patience — reload inside the station to re-roll.
2. If it was found at a **trading outpost**, fly to the outpost and run the reload loop there instead.
3. Scan every arriving ship. When the system's exotic lands — it will be the model from the post — buy it.

Check the galaxy before you dial. Addresses are galaxy-specific: a Euclid address dialed from Eissentam lands you somewhere random and exotic-less.

## Sharing Your Own Exotic Coordinates

Posting a find that actually works takes four things:

1. **The glyph address.** Go to any portal, request the portal's address, and screenshot the **12 glyphs** — that screenshot is your post's payload. Type the glyph names or numbers out in text as well, since screenshots age badly.
2. **The galaxy.** Euclid, Eissentam, or wherever — without it the address is unusable.
3. **The exact location.** Space station or trading outpost, and the planet name if it is an outpost find.
4. **A stat screenshot.** The scanned ship panel showing class, damage, shields, hyperdrive, and slots. This is what separates real posts from noise.

Cross-platform play means you do not need to worry about who can use it — an address posted from PlayStation works for a Steam player in the same galaxy. ## How to Spot a Fake or Useless Coordinate Post

Coordinate posts fail in predictable ways. Learn the patterns and you will waste far less time at other people's outposts:

| Red flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| No galaxy named | The address cannot be verified or used from the wrong galaxy |
| Fewer than 12 glyphs | Not a complete address — portals need all 12 slots |
| "God rolls" with no stat screenshot | Unverifiable; real hunters screenshot the scan panel |
| Post predates a major update | Wave behavior may have shifted; expect longer hunts, not failure |
| Outpost vs. station unclear | You cannot run the right loop if you are in the wrong place |
| Reposted through several aggregators with broken formatting | Glyph names garble easily in transit; find the original post |

Two habits close the loop. First, prefer posts with a stat screenshot and a date. Second, remember the retry math: because a system's exotic is fixed, a legitimate address that fails once has not failed permanently — it just needs more reloads. The failure mode that actually matters is being in the wrong galaxy.
