Every new No Man's Sky system has one item everyone googles the day they find it. For the Xeno Arena's creature battles, that item is the **Retroviral Pellet** — the gene-altering consumable that upgrades your creature's battle traits. This page covers what it actually does, where more come from, and the strategy for spending them, without padding. The short version: pellets are the optimization tool, not the progression requirement — and they are scarce enough that spending them well is the whole game.

**Last updated: August 27, 2026.** Details below reflect the Xeno Arena system as it stands in game version 6.5; the [patch notes page](/cosmos-update/cosmos-patch-notes/) is the source of record if Hello Games tunes drop rates or adds crafting.

## What Retroviral Pellets Actually Do

Hello Games' own description is the cleanest one: pellets are **gene-altering**, and they exist to **upgrade the battle traits of creature companions more efficiently**. Unpack that into the two layers of the creature battle system:

- **Levels** come from fighting. Creatures earn experience for participating, all the way to the cap. Pellets have nothing to do with this.
- **Traits** are the battler's toolkit — the abilities and characteristics that decide whether a creature is a puncher, a tank, or a liability. Random at first. **This is the layer pellets edit.**

The full battle system — turn structure, the Arena League ladder, Oceanus's daily challenge — is covered in the [pet battling guide](/guides/pet-battling-guide/). This page stays on the item.

## How to Get Them

Two sources, one guaranteed and one grind:

1. **Beat Oceanus.** Your first win against the Space Anomaly's arena trainer awards a pellet, and the game immediately suggests what to do with it. Every battler's first pellet is free.
2. **Win trainer battles.** After that, pellets drop as **occasional** rewards from beating NPC creature trainers — the keyword being occasional. They are a drip, not a faucet.

And the non-source: **no crafting recipe exists** as of version 6.5. Player communities have looked; nothing verified has come back. If a recipe appears in a future patch, the patch notes page linked above will be where it lands first.

The practical consequence: pellets are a **budget**. You will never have as many as you want, which makes the spending decision — the next section — the actual skill.

## When to Spend One

The worst use of a pellet is the tempting one: the moment you get it, on whatever creature you currently like. Don't. The drip is too slow to waste on placeholders.

Spend on a **keeper**, which in this system means:

- **Strong base stats.** Levels and traits can move; a creature's underlying stat quality is what you are building on. The daily-challenge grind is also how you [earn nanites](/guides/nanites-farming/) alongside, so the arena doubles as income while you evaluate your roster.
- **A role worth patching.** One great stat creature with a patched trait lineup beats six wild rolls — the same roster philosophy as the [2026 tips list](/guides/tips-and-tricks-2026/) preaches for every system in the game.
- **Breeding stock.** Community-reported practice: serious breeders keep several similarly leveled creatures and use pellets on the parents they intend to breed from, so the upgrades propagate. Treat this as community-tested wisdom rather than official documentation.

The pellet flow and the roster strategy are the same decision: build deliberately, edit deliberately.

## Pellet Strategy Checklist

- ✅ Beat Oceanus first — guaranteed pellet, and the tutorial fight
- ✅ Win trainer battles regularly — the only renewable source
- ❌ Don't pellet early catches you will replace
- ❌ Don't hold them forever either — an unspent pellet optimizes nothing
- ✅ Pellet the keeper: strong stats, clear role, breeding candidate

## The One-Sentence Version

Retroviral Pellets upgrade what your creature *is* rather than how high its level is; you get them from beating Oceanus once and trainer battles occasionally; and the entire skill is spending them on a creature worth keeping.
