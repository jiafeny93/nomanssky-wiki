A base in No Man's Sky is two projects wearing one roof: the architecture you show off, and the power grid underneath that decides whether any of it works. The teleporter, the refiners, the storage containers, every lamp — all of it draws from one electrical system, and the system is simple enough to master in an evening and generous enough to automate entire bases. This is that evening.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For where to place the base in the first place, see [Base Computer placement](/base-building/base-computer-placement/); for the building basics, the [base building guide](/base-building/base-building-guide/).

## The Grid in One Paragraph

Every base has one power grid. **Generators** feed it, **batteries** buffer it, **consumers** draw from it, and **wire** connects them. No circuits, no per-room wiring — one source can power the whole base, and the build menu shows each part's power state live. A powered part glows ready; an unpowered one sulks in the dark and does nothing. That is the entire model.

## The Generator Ladder

| Source | Needs | Runs | Best at |
|--------|-------|------|---------|
| Solar Panel | Sunlight | Daylight only | Small and mid bases, anywhere |
| Biofuel Generator | Carbon fuel | While fueled | Early game, backup, oxygen-of-attention builds |
| Electromagnetic Generator | An EM hotspot | Always, no fuel | Everything, once you have a hotspot |

**Solar panels** are the honest default: free fuel, silent operation, daylight hours only. Pair them with batteries and they carry a base indefinitely. They are the right answer for teleporter shacks, farm outposts, and every base until you find something better.

**Biofuel generators** burn carbon-family fuel for steady output regardless of weather or hour. They are the bridge technology — useful in the first hours when you have carbon and no research, and useful forever as the generator you toss down at a temporary work site.

**Electromagnetic generators** are the endgame answer. On a planet with an electromagnetic hotspot — found with the Survey Device on your multitool — an EM generator produces day and night, storm and calm, with zero fuel and zero attention. One strong hotspot powers a megabase. The catch is geography: the hotspot is where it is, so the base moves to it or runs long wire.

The practical ladder most players climb: **panel + battery → panels + more batteries → EM generators on a hotspot**, with biofuel covering gaps at every stage.

## Batteries: The Night Shift

Batteries store surplus generation and discharge when supply dips. The sizing rule is simple: **enough battery capacity to carry your consumers through the night**. Undersized banks show up as lamps and teleporters that die at 2 a.m. planet time — annoying, never destructive; nothing breaks when a base browns out, it all just stops until dawn or refuel.

Build batteries near the generators, wire them in, and the grid handles the rest. Bigger bases do not need cleverer battery logic, just more of them.

## Wiring Without the Nest

Power lines snap to sockets on parts and can be re-anchored and rerouted at will. Clean wiring is a habit, not a feature:

1. **Backbone first.** One main line from the source toward the base's functional core; branches tap off the backbone.
2. **Follow architecture.** Cable along ceiling lines, skirting, and wall corners reads as designed; cable strung diagonally across rooms reads as panic.
3. **Bury what cannot be pretty.** Lines that must cross open space can run under floors or inside terrain — mound earth over the run with the Terrain Manipulator and it vanishes.
4. **Re-anchor, do not re-string.** Moving an existing wire's endpoints is free; deleting and rebuilding is busywork.

## Switches and Sensors: Cheap Automation

Two small parts turn a grid from plumbing into behavior:

- **Wall switch** — toggles power to whatever sits downstream of it. Manual control for rooms you want dark on demand.
- **Proximity sensor** — powers its connected parts while a player is in range. The classic: sensor on the entrance light or door, and the base greets you without a button.

Chain them with the logic parts from the same research family and bases get genuinely clever — lighting that follows you room to room, farm machinery that only runs when you visit. Most builders stop at motion lighting and are perfectly happy; the ceiling is taste, not tech.

## Power and the Base Checklist

A grid is healthy when all three lines hold:

- **Generation covers peak draw** — everything running at once, storms included.
- **Storage covers the dark hours** — batteries sized to the night, not to the afternoon.
- **Consumers are connected** — a part that looks dead is almost always an unwired part; check its highlight state before its research state.

## Common Power Mistakes

- **Forgetting the battery.** A solar-only base works beautifully right up until sunset, and the teleporter is the first thing you notice.
- **Building first, surveying never.** A base raised on a strong EM hotspot never thinks about power again; a base raised three hundred meters from one thinks about power forever. Survey before the foundation — it is the single most consequential power decision, and it costs one scan.
- **Wiring across the yard.** Consumers far from source mean long, fragile, ugly runs. Cluster the functional core near generation, or move generation to the core.
- **Powering decoration you never see.** Every lamp on the grid is a lamp on the night budget. Motion sensors fix exactly this.

The quiet truth of base power: once an EM hotspot is involved, the entire system disappears from your life. Until then, panels and batteries ask one evening of care. Either way, the lights come on when you teleport home — and after a long expedition, that is the whole point of [having the base](/base-building/base-building-tips/) at all.
