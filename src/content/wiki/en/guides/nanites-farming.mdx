Units buy ships, freighters, and fleets. Nanites buy power. Every upgrade module worth installing, every blueprint worth owning, and every class upgrade that turns a good hull or multitool into a great one is priced in nanites, and no merchant in the galaxy will sell you nanites for units. If your build has stalled because the technology merchant wants 850 nanites you do not have, you need a farm, not a trade route.

Nanite farming in No Man's Sky splits into seven distinct schools, from a near-afk refiner loop to lawless black-market arbitrage, and they suit completely different saves. This guide covers all of them with the exact refining ratios, ranked by what an hour of each actually returns against its entry barrier and risk. **Last updated: September 1, 2026.** Nothing in the staged Cosmos rollout has changed any mechanic below as of this writing; see the [Cosmos update guide](/cosmos-update/cosmos-update-guide) for what has actually shipped this month.

## What Nanites Actually Buy

Before farming, know the sink. Nanites pay for technology merchant stock on every space station, where class-rated upgrade modules for ships, exosuits, and multitools are the staple purchase, with S-class modules carrying the biggest price tags and the best stats. They pay blueprint traders for crafting recipes. They pay the class upgrades that promote a C-class multitool or starship toward S-class at an outfitting terminal, five-figure projects that are the largest nanite sink in most saves. They also cover quieter costs: squadron slots for your freighter, extra companion pet slots, freighter paint options, and the payments Travellers ask for grave locations. If you want the full picture of the units side of the economy, our [money making guide](/guides/money-making-guide) covers it; nanites are the currency that units cannot touch.

The top three are production farms; the bottom four are steady income you collect while playing the game normally. Now the detail, starting with the method that out-earns everything per hour of effort at almost no cost.

## The Runaway Mould Loop (Core Method)

Runaway Mould is a junk-tier substance that refines straight into nanite clusters at the best sustained rate of anything you can gather for free. The wiki-documented recipe, unchanged for years:

| Input | Output | Operation | Refiner speed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Runaway Mould ×5 | Nanite Cluster ×1 | Recycle Waste Materials | 0.6 sec per nanite |

Five mould in, one nanite out, one nanite finished every 0.6 seconds. That is 100 nanites a minute, 6,000 an hour, per refiner. Mould stacks to 9,999, so a full stack becomes about 2,000 nanites in roughly 20 minutes per refiner, and the finished nanites convert to currency instantly without occupying inventory slots. Run four refiners at once and the same 20 minutes produces about 8,000 nanites.

The mould comes from Curious Deposits: clusters of glowing spheres sitting on planetary surfaces, visible as a scan target through your Analysis Visor from the air. Land, mine the balls with your mining beam, and the deposit stays empty, until you force a respawn. Here is the complete closed loop:

1. **Find a deposit.** Scan planets from your ship for Curious Deposit markers, or use farm coordinates shared by communities like the Galactic Hub, which publish addresses of dense, tested deposits.
2. **Build the farm.** Drop a base computer at the deposit, then a Base Teleport Module and as many Medium Refiners as you can place beside the balls. The Medium Refiner blueprint comes from the construction research terminal on the Space Anomaly.
3. **Mine the first pass.** A rich deposit yields well over 100,000 mould an hour of active mining, community-tested, so 15 to 20 minutes of mining fills every refiner slot and your exosuit.
4. **Load the refiners.** Each refiner input slot holds a full 9,999 stack. Split your haul across all of them; they run in parallel in real time.
5. **Force the respawn.** Teleport to a station or another base, then teleport straight back. Community testing says you must be more than roughly 600 units away from the deposit for the respawn to trigger. The balls return in full, ready to mine again while the refiners keep working. Reloading a restore point at a save beacon works too.
6. **Collect on the cycle.** Every 20 minutes or so, each refiner finishes a stack into about 2,000 nanites. Top them up with freshly mined mould and keep looping.

A one-hour session at a good deposit with three or four refiners running realistically nets 20,000 to 30,000 nanites, and long community sessions with more refiners report past 40,000 an hour during the gathering phase. The refining is the bottleneck, not the mining, which is why the farm design puts refiners at the deposit rather than in your freighter. Because the deposit respawns forever, this is the only top-tier farm with essentially infinite supply and zero inputs beyond your time.

One footnote for the refining database-minded: Runaway Mould is the end of a longer chain. Residual Goop refines to Viscous Fluids to Living Slime to Runaway Mould, each step 1:1, before the final 5:1 into nanites. Buying goop cheap from station vendors and walking it up the chain works, but the chain is slow; the deposit loop exists precisely to skip it.

## The Whispering Egg Route

The mould loop's darker sibling: no base, no refiner bank, one scan target and a portable refiner. Every abandoned building on a planetary surface is ringed by whispering eggs, and each egg cracks into a larval core worth 50 nanites in a refiner — the richest free pickup in the gathering set. The catch is everything that comes with it.

The gathering routes, side by side:

| Input | Nanites out | Where to gather |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runaway Mould ×5 | 1 | Curious Deposits, scanned from the air |
| Whispering Egg (as a Larval Core) | 50 | Abandoned Buildings |
| Hadal Core | 50 | Underwater ruin sites |
| Salvaged Data | 15 | Buried technology modules |
| Tainted Metal | 2 | Corrupted worlds and derelict freighters |
| Pugneum ×25 | 1 | Sentinel kills |

The run itself: sweep a planet from the air until the Analysis Visor flags an Abandoned Building, then crack eggs one at a time. Cores left on the ground decay within seconds, so crack, grab, and move. Every crack summons a biological horror swarm, and the community playbook for surviving it is short — hide inside the building until the wave loses interest, park an exocraft next to the eggs and hop in between grabs, or take three cores and bolt for the ship. One building's full cluster is worth roughly 1,000 to 2,000 nanites, community-cited, and community farmers report the eggs reset on a save reload, so a save at the building turns it into a repeatable stop on any route. Derelict freighters carry a few cores in their final room too, but the community verdict is that the building circuit beats them many times over for the effort. The complete list of what refines into nanites is tabled in our [refining recipes guide](/guides/refining-recipes).

## Ship Scrapping: Units Into Nanites

Every station has a Starship Outfitting Terminal that dismantles ships you own, and the returns are nanite-denominated. The community-tested rules: scrapping yields one to five upgrade modules, with module grade matching the ship's class, and each module sells to technology merchants for 30 to 1,300 nanites depending on class. You also recover roughly 75 percent of the ship's price in sellable materials, with the caveat that dumping salvage in the same system economy twice crashes its price by as much as 80 percent, so sell across systems.

The loop is simple. Sit in a wealthy 3-star system where better-class ships land, buy cheap arrivals, scrap them, sell the modules, and repeat. With a bankroll of 75 to 200 million units, the community puts this at upwards of 30,000 nanites an hour, the highest verified ceiling of any method. Higher-class ships also roll Storage Augmentations more often; the full scrapping mechanics and how to combine them with an S-class hunt are in our [S-class ships guide](/starships/s-class-ships), which covers that loop end to end.

## Pirate Stations and Suspicious Packets

Outlaw stations in pirate systems run the other great units-to-nanites exchange. The scrap dealer stocks suspicious packets of tech and arms, about five of each per restock, at roughly 74,000 units apiece, or about 320 Tainted Metal. Each packet opens into one random X-class upgrade module. Install the good rolls and sell the rest to technology merchants back on regular stations; unwanted modules convert to nanites on the spot.

The method is fast, safe, and capped only by dealer stock, which restocks on an irregular timer, so serious farmers route between multiple outlaw systems. Community-cited runs put it around 20,000 to 25,000 nanites an hour while stock lasts. You need a Conflict Scanner or galaxy-map filtering to find pirate systems efficiently, plus the unit budget to buy in bulk; the units side of that budget is what our [money making guide](/guides/money-making-guide) is for.

## Derelict Freighters: Loot and Tainted Metal

Procedural horror freighters, boarded using an Emergency Signal Scanner bought from the station scrap dealer for 5 million units (the price climbs with same-day purchases and resets daily; Iteration Helios on the Anomaly gives one free receiver a week), are a nanite farm that also pays units. A typical run yields the equivalent of roughly 2,500 to 3,500 nanites plus 2 to 6 million units in salvage, a Cargo Bulkhead or Salvaged Fleet Unit for your own freighter, and a hold full of Tainted Metal, which refines into nanites at a wiki-documented 1:2.

The nanite sources inside: cargo containers and lockers scattered through the hull, upgrade modules pulled from terminals, and the data logs, which sell to the scrap dealer for nanites or guild standing. Runs take 20 to 30 minutes, hazards and hostile biology included, so the hourly rate lands below the top farms, but nothing else pays nanites, units, and fleet upgrades in the same half hour. Freighter basics are in our [freighter guide](/starships/freighter-guide).

## Nexus Missions: The Steady Drip

The Nexus aboard the Space Anomaly posts rotating missions, and its standard offerings pay out a few hundred nanites each, community reports cluster in the 150 to 400 range, alongside standing. Two mechanical notes make it better than it looks: the reward is randomly nanites or units per mission, so if the board offers units, abandon and re-take until nanite missions appear; and every completion also feeds the daily 400 Quicksilver mission and guild standing, which the [Quicksilver farming guide](/guides/quicksilver-farming) covers separately.

At a few thousand nanites an hour this will never compete with a mould farm, but it is combat-free, system-agnostic income you can run in your first hours of play. When an [expedition](/expeditions/expedition-guide) is live, its milestone tracks have regularly included nanite rewards too, so a returning save can double-dip.

## Sentinel Hunting: Combat Income

Sentinels pay two ways. Destroyed drones drop Combat Supplies, which yield nanites directly alongside Salvaged Glass, and each Salvaged Glass opens into a free upgrade module you can sell. The drops also include Pugneum, which refines at a wiki-documented 25:1, and rare drops like Hyaline Brain, worth 230 nanites refined. In space, Sentinel Interceptors shot down around a station drop nanites outright plus those same brains and glass.

The community wiki rates a well-executed combat loop as rivaling ship scrapping for income. The recipe on the ground: provoke a planet with aggressive sentinels to Wanted level 3, keep the summoner drones alive so waves keep coming, and loot Combat Supplies remotely without breaking position. The barrier is real, a combat-built multitool and comfort under fire, and the risk is the point; this is the farm for players who want the fight to be the content.

## Words, Knowledge Stones, and Discovery Uploads

The faintest school, worth setting straight because it is widely misremembered. Knowledge stones themselves teach vocabulary, not nanites; the wiki is explicit that a stone grants a word of the local language and nothing else. What words actually buy is dialogue: the more of a species' language you know, the more options unlock at monoliths and station NPCs, and those conversations do sometimes pay nanites among their rewards.

The real exploration income is discovery data. Uploading scans pays nanites on every planet, and completing the fauna roster of a planet pays a completion bonus that community testing puts at up to roughly 4,750 nanites, averaging around 2,000, on top of per-creature upload rewards with an upgraded scanner. Add Salvaged Data from buried technology modules, refineable at 1:15, and exploration quietly funds a new save's first module purchases. If you are days one through three of a fresh file, this drip plus Nexus missions is the intended diet; our [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide) sequences it.

## Which Farm Fits Your Save

| Your situation | Best farm | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First hours, no capital | Nexus missions + discovery uploads + Salvaged Data | Zero barriers, runs from the Anomaly |
| Mid save, units tight | Runaway mould loop | Free inputs, best rate, one-time setup |
| Unit millionaire | Ship scrapping or suspicious packets | Convert surplus money at the highest ceilings |
| Combat build | Sentinel hunting | Income plus the fight itself |
| Freighter progression | Derelict freighters | Nanites, units, and fleet upgrades together |

Most saves converge on the mould loop eventually, because it is the only farm that scales without consuming anything. Build it once, and every future S-class module is 20 minutes of teleport cycles away.
