Every Sentinel Interceptor in No Man's Sky starts life as a wreck. You cannot buy one at a space station, order one from a scrap dealer, or find one parked at a trading outpost; the only path is to land on a corrupted planet, work your way into a harmonic camp, and repair the crashed ship that the camp's terminal points you toward. The chain is short once you know it, but the game explains almost none of it, which is exactly why help threads about these ships keep filling up on Reddit year after year. **Last updated: August 17, 2026** — the system below has stayed functionally unchanged from the Interceptor update of 2023 through Worlds, Voyagers, Remnant, and into the current Cosmos era, so this is how it works today.

## What Is a Sentinel Interceptor?

The Sentinel Interceptor — also called a Sentinel Ship or Salvaged Ship — is a starship class introduced in the Interceptor update (April 2023). These are the ships the Sentinel fleet flies: angular, insect-like hulls that can carry up to nine or more engine nozzles at the rear, and that hum with their own technology line instead of the standard one.

The closest comparison is the Living Ship. Both replace the normal core technology with a bespoke set of systems, but the Interceptor is far more flexible: regular starship technology and upgrade modules can be installed alongside the Sentinel hardware, so you are not locked out of the module economy the way Organic Ships are.

## Why Players Hunt Them

Three properties do most of the recruiting.

First, Interceptors are the cheapest ships in the game to operate. They never consume Starship Launch Fuel or Warp Cells. Their launch and warp systems recharge with **Radiant Shards**, which dissonant planets supply in effectively unlimited quantities, so a stocked Interceptor is a ship you never refuel again.

Second, they are free. Every Interceptor is claimed from a crash site and repaired with on-planet materials, which makes them one of the few endgame ship types that costs time rather than units.

Third, their technology equivalents are direct swaps for the normal systems, so nothing about your build habits has to change:

| Interceptor technology | Replaces | Recharging |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anti-Gravity Well | Launch Thrusters | Radiant Shards, not launch fuel |
| Luminance Engine | Pulse Engine | Tritium, as usual |
| Crimson Core | Hyperdrive | Radiant Shards, not warp cells |
| Sentinel Cannon | Photon Cannon | Charged as usual |
| Aeron Shield | Deflector Shield | Charged as usual |

Interceptor wrecks also roll real stat spreads, from C to S class, and squadron pilots can fly the ones you do not keep — which turns harmonic camps into a farming loop rather than a one-time visit.

## Step 1: Find a Dissonant System and Its Corrupted Planet

1. Open the galaxy map and look for a system tagged **Dissonant**. No special scanner is required; the tag appears alongside economy and conflict information.
2. Warp in and scan the planets from space. In a dissonant system, the dissonant planet shows **Corrupted Sentinels** where the sentinel activity rating normally sits.
3. Land anywhere on that planet. The surface is where every material and every step of this guide lives.

Dissonant planets are their own biome type: pink-hued, Atlantideum-rich, and patrolled by corrupted Sentinel drones instead of the standard fleet. Everything you need — shards, mirrors, camps, wrecks — exists only here, so set up your exocraft and inventory space before committing.

## Step 2: Locate a Harmonic Camp

Harmonic camps exist only on dissonant planets. They are small Autophage-built installations anchored by a locked terminal, and there are three reliable ways to find one:

1. **Echo Locator (the standard route).** Destroy Corrupted Sentinel drones — including the quadrupeds that patrol these planets — until one drops an **Echo Locator**. Use it from your inventory and the nearest harmonic camp is marked on your HUD.
2. **Exploration.** Camps occasionally surface as survey icons while you roam on foot or in an exocraft, no locator required.
3. **Skip the camp entirely.** Defeating **wave 5** of a corrupted Sentinel patrol reveals a dissonance spike — a direct crash-site marker — and the Carrier AI Fragment item (dropped by destroyed Sentinel capital ships, or occasionally from Nexus missions) also returns a crash location.

There is also a one-off scripted variant: a distressed Sentinel ship can appear as a space encounter, broadcast a garbled plea, and warp away leaving a trail to its own crash site. Whichever route you take, the destination is the same kind of wreck.

## Step 3: Unlock the Camp Terminal

The camp terminal is gated by a small override puzzle rather than combat:

1. Clear the corrupted drones around the camp. They respawn, so repair and recharge between waves if needed.
2. Interact with the terminal and read the screen. It shows four glyph slots, reveals two of the four values as plain numbers, and gives you either the **sum or the product** of all four.
3. Work out the two missing numbers, then enter the glyphs that match them. The glyph strip counts from zero — the first glyph is 0, the second is 1, and so on — so a value of 14 means the fifteenth glyph.
4. With the override accepted, the terminal's dialogue opens up, including the option you came for: **Locate Dissonance Spikes**.

Before you leave, drop a manual save at the camp (or a Save Point next to the terminal). This is the anchor for the farming method covered below, and it saves you from re-solving the puzzle on every visit.

## Step 4: Follow the Dissonance Spike to the Wreck

Select the locate option and a dissonance spike is plotted on your HUD. Fly or drive to it. The crash site has three features: the wrecked Interceptor itself, an inactive Autophage unit standing nearby — pure lore bait for now, though feeding it Atlantideum yields cryptic binary messages — and the interactables that begin the repair chain.

Interact with the wreck first. Claiming it requires stripping the damaged components, which converts the ship into a repair project in your inventory. On rare occasions a wreck can be claimed immediately with no repair chain, but plan on the full process.

## Repair Checklist: Every Material You Need

The repair chain is where most players stall, because two of the materials do not exist anywhere near a normal space station.

| Item | Role | How to get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pugneum | Acquired at the site | Dropped by destroyed Sentinel drones anywhere in the galaxy |
| Salvaged Glass | Acquired at the site | Dropped by destroyed drones; opens into upgrade modules and alloys |
| Hyaline Brain | Acquired at the site | Interact with it, and it will point you to a nearby monolith |
| Harmonic Brain | Conversion of the Hyaline Brain | Complete the monolith interaction the brain leads you to |
| **Radiant Shard ×3** | Repairs the Pilot Interface | Dropped by destroyed corrupted drones, plentiful across the surface |
| **Inverted Mirror ×1** | Repairs the Pilot Interface | Dropped by the heavier corrupted units, such as quadrupeds and walkers |
| Remaining damaged systems | Engines, shields, weapons | Standard starship materials (Tritium, Metal Plating, and the like) or one Repair Kit per slot |

The sequence that matters:

1. Collect the Pugneum, Salvaged Glass, and Hyaline Brain from the wreck.
2. Farm **3 Radiant Shards** and **1 Inverted Mirror** from the corrupted patrols.
3. Interact with the Hyaline Brain. It marks a monolith; the game marks the Interceptor so you can find your way back.
4. Convert the brain into the Harmonic Brain at the monolith.
5. Return to the wreck and repair the Pilot Interface with the shards, the mirror, and the Harmonic Brain.
6. Repair the remaining damaged systems, then claim the ship.

One warning worth heeding: because Interceptor repairs consume corrupted-planet resources, a fully broken Interceptor on a non-dissonant world can strand you. That matters most after a galaxy jump, which arrives with wrecked ships. Carry **Repair Kits** — they substitute for any single slot's materials — before any intergalactic travel.

## What You Get Once It Flies

Beyond the fuel economy, a claimed Interceptor behaves like any other ship in your collection. Normal upgrade modules and technologies install alongside the Sentinel systems, so the usual supercharged-slot adjacency planning applies. Stat-wise they are solid all-rounders rather than category specialists, and the ceiling depends on the class roll of the wreck you claimed.

The hull variation is system-bound: the exact aesthetic a crash site yields is tied to the system you found it in. Repeated dissonance spikes in the same system always produce the same-looking ship with different stats. Want a different silhouette? Bring the farming loop to a different dissonant system.

## Farming Tips: Classes, Stats, and Scrapping

Because stats reroll while looks stay fixed per system, the optimization loop is straightforward:

1. Find a dissonant system whose Interceptor style you like and unlock its harmonic camp.
2. Make your manual save at the camp terminal.
3. Locate a spike, fly to the wreck, and check the class and stat spread before repairing anything beyond the claim requirements.
4. If the roll is poor, reload the camp save and locate a new spike. The wreck rerolls; the silhouette does not.
5. Claim and repair keepers; claim and scrap the rest. Even C-class Interceptors scrap for enough units and nanite-adjacent returns that the loop pays for itself.
