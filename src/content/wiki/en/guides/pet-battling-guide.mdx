For ten years, No Man's Sky creatures were scenery: things you scanned, occasionally rode, and photographed at sunset. The **Xeno Arena update** changed their job description. Adopt a creature now and it can fight — Pokémon-style, turn-based, with a hundred-plus abilities, a genetics lab, a daily worldwide challenge, and an Arena League to climb. This guide covers the whole system: adopting your roster, building a team, the genetics layer, and the daily Oceanus fight that structures every battler's day.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For the taming basics that feed this system, see the [starter tips](/guides/starter-tips/); this page assumes you can already adopt companions and want the battle layer.

## What Xeno Arena Added

The update's battle system in one list:

- **Turn-based creature battles** — your team versus an opponent's, with cinematic cameras, new music, and a combat log.
- **100+ abilities** across creatures, earned and modified through play.
- **A genetics interface** for editing companion creatures — abilities and traits are not fixed at birth.
- **Breeding and training** — raise creatures deliberately rather than only catching them.
- **The Arena League** — a progression ladder of challengers with rewards.
- **Iteration: Oceanus** — the Space Anomaly NPC who gates the system and issues a **daily battle, the same one for every player in the world that day**.
- **The tamed-creature cap raised to thirty** — room for a real roster.

## Step 1: Build the Roster

Battling starts with adoption. Out in the wild, creatures are tamed with the food they respond to — the long-standing companion system that predates the Arena. Every adopted creature is a potential fighter, and the cap of thirty tamed creatures means your planetary zoo is now a bench.

What makes a good battler is the same thing that makes a good collection: variety. Abilities differ across species, so a roster of six very different creatures — different roles, different damage shapes, different tricks — beats six of your favorite. You are not looking for one champion; you are looking for answers.

## Step 2: Find Oceanus at the Anomaly

The system's front door is the **Space Anomaly**. Speak to **Iteration: Oceanus**, who serves as instructor, first opponent, and gatekeeper to the Arena League. The introductory fights teach the turn structure; the League beyond them is the progression system, with challengers to beat and rewards along the way.

The daily structure is the hook: each day, Oceanus issues one unique battle, identical for every player worldwide. Beat it and the win counts toward milestones — the long chase being the **30-victory challenge**, alongside raising creatures to the level cap of 30. One note from the community: the daily is normally once per day, but players have found that warping to a new galaxy resets the counter — the dedicated grind it out that way.

## Step 3: Understand the Battle Layer

The combat is deliberately legible: turn-based exchanges where your creatures' abilities resolve against the opponent's team, with a combat log making every exchange auditable after the fact. Depth comes from the roster construction and ability layer rather than execution — this is a strategy system, not an action one.

The practical skills:

1. **Read the day's challenge before picking the team.** A fixed daily battle is a puzzle with a global answer — the community solves each day's Oceanus fight collectively, and checking what worked is not cheating, it is the meta.
2. **Cover roles, not favorites.** Damage, survivability, and utility abilities each win different fights; a balanced six answers more dailies than six glass cannons.
3. **Level deliberately.** The level cap of 30 defines the ceiling of the daily fights; a full roster at cap makes every daily a team-building question rather than a power question.

## Step 4: Use the Genetics System

The differentiator between a collector and a battler: **genetics**. The genetics interface lets you modify the abilities and traits of your companions, and **Retroviral Pellets** are the consumable route to the same edits — feed them to a creature and reroll or upgrade what nature dealt it.

The strategic shape: wild adoption gives you raw material, genetics turns material into a moveset. A creature with a great stat profile and a mediocre ability becomes your best fighter after pellet editing; a perfect-ability creature gets its weaknesses patched. The 100+ ability pool means the editing surface is deep — plan which creature carries which answer before spending pellets.

## Step 5: Climb and Collect

Beyond the dailies, the **Arena League** structures long-term play: challengers in sequence, rewards at milestones, and the training/breeding loop feeding it all. The rewards are real but sane — this is not the game's best [units farm](/guides/units-farming/), and it does not need to be. The payoff structure is a collection game's: milestones, exclusives, and the satisfaction of a roster you built.

## Who This System Is For

The honest segmentation: if your favorite part of No Man's Sky was always the fauna — the scanning, the taming, the riding — Xeno Arena is your endgame, and the genetics layer will eat your evenings. If you are here for ships and [combat builds](/multitools/weapon-modes-guide/), the Arena is a well-made side system worth one evening and the daily habit. And if you are returning after years away: yes, the game has Pokémon battles now, the daily is genuinely fun, and Oceanus is waiting at the Anomaly with today's puzzle.
