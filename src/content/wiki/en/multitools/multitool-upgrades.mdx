A stock multitool is a blank chassis. The thing that mines asteroids of gold, melts pirate squadrons, and turns a walk through the woods into a units faucet is not the tool you bought — it is the upgrade layer you installed on it. And the upgrade system rewards players who understand it disproportionately: two tools of identical class and type can differ by half again in performance purely from module choice, placement, and slot discipline.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. This is the system guide — how modules, slots, and bonuses interact. For what to install on a specific tool, see [best multitools](/multitools/best-multitools/), [weapon modes](/multitools/weapon-modes-guide/), or [mining beam builds](/multitools/mining-beam-upgrades/).

## The Three Layers of a Multitool Build

| Layer | What it is | Where it comes from |
|-------|------------|---------------------|
| Core technologies | The base Mining Beam, Scanner, Boltcaster, etc. | Installed by default or crafted from blueprints |
| Upgrade modules | Class-graded boosters per family (C/B/A/S) | Technology merchants — stations and the Anomaly, priced in [nanites](/guides/nanites-farming/) |
| Slots | Technology slots vs general slots; supercharged slots | Ships with the tool; expandable at station cabinets and the Anomaly |

Technology slots hold tech and modules; general slots hold anything, at reduced tech efficiency — a fine overflow valve, never the plan. Supercharged slots are the highlighted technology slots every tool carries a few of, and they are the most valuable real estate on the grid.

## Rule One: Three Modules Per Family, Then Stop

Each technology family — Mining Beam, Boltcaster, Scanner, and so on — accepts its core technology plus **up to three upgrade modules**. The fourth module of the same family contributes nothing: no stat gain, one fewer slot. Every properly built tool is therefore an exercise in choosing families, not stacking one:

- **A combat tool** takes three modules of its main weapon family (boltcaster or neutron cannon, per [weapon modes](/multitools/weapon-modes-guide/)), plus three of a backup or utility family.
- **A scanner tool** takes three Scanner Upgrades plus its analysis tech — the build that makes an [experimental](/multitools/experimental-multitool/) print money.
- **A mining tool** takes three Mining Beam Upgrades plus heat management tech — the deep dive is [mining beam upgrades](/multitools/mining-beam-upgrades/).

The limit is why slot counts matter less than players fear: three families times three modules plus cores fits comfortably in a good tool's technology grid.

## Rule Two: Adjacency Is Free Performance

Modules of the same family perform better when placed **adjacent to their core technology and to each other**. The game communicates this the moment you move a module — stats shift live in the install screen. The discipline it asks for:

1. Install the core technology.
2. Cluster its three modules in the surrounding slots, touching the core and each other.
3. Keep families in contiguous blocks — a "checkerboard" grid of scattered modules is the most common self-inflicted nerf in the game.

Plan the grid before you install: cores and their module blocks first, supercharged slots reserved, general slots left for overflow.

## Rule Three: Supercharged Slots Go to Your Identity

Supercharged slots multiply whatever sits in them. The allocation question — "which single module most defines this tool?" — has a build-specific answer:

- **Scanner tool:** the strongest Scanner Upgrade. This is the difference between a good payout and an absurd one.
- **Combat tool:** the strongest damage module of your primary weapon family.
- **Hybrid/royal-style builds:** the family you actually use most — resist spreading supercharged boosts across two families unless you genuinely split time evenly.

## Buying Modules: Where and How

Technology merchants on **space stations** sell module stock for nanites, refreshed per system — and, as with everything, **wealthy systems stock better classes**. The **Space Anomaly's** multitool merchant is the guaranteed baseline stock when a route disappoints. Practical shopping:

1. Bring nanites — hundreds for A-class, more for S-class stock. Our [nanite farming guide](/guides/nanites-farming/) covers the standard earning loops.
2. Check the class letter on offer per family before buying; a C-class module is placeholder gear, S-class is the destination.
3. Buy duplicate families across systems when hunting a better roll — module stats vary within a class band, so veterans replace good modules with slightly better good modules.

## Two Reference Builds

**The money scanner** (on an [experimental](/multitools/experimental-multitool/)):

| Grid region | Contents |
|-------------|----------|
| Supercharged slots | Strongest Scanner Upgrade |
| Technology block 1 | Scanner core + remaining Scanner Upgrades, clustered |
| Technology block 2 | Mining Beam core + three Mining Beam Upgrades (the working tool still has to mine) |
| General slots | Shield and utility overflow |

**The dissonance fighter** (on a [sentinel multitool](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/)):

| Grid region | Contents |
|-------------|----------|
| Supercharged slots | Strongest primary damage module |
| Technology block 1 | Primary weapon core (boltcaster or neutron cannon) + three upgrades, clustered |
| Technology block 2 | Mining Beam line for the resource loop |
| General slots | Health/shield tech overflow, teleporter utility |

## Mistakes That Cost Real Performance

- **Scattering modules** and forfeiting adjacency — the silent, universal error.
- **Installing a fourth same-family module** — pure slot waste.
- **Supercharging a utility** instead of your build's identity module.
- **Buying a prettier tool before upgrading the current one** — an A-class tool with disciplined modules beats an S-class tool with none, a point worth internalizing before any [royal hunt](/multitools/royal-multitool/).
- **Neglecting the scanner** on general-purpose tools — scanner modules pay for all the other modules.

The upgrade layer is the same across every tool type in the game, which is the good news: learn it once, and every tool you ever buy — station, outlaw, or [harmonic camp](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/) — gets built right the day you buy it.
