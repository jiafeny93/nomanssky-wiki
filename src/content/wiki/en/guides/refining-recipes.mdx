No Man's Sky has two economies. The one you can see — units, ships, shopping — and the one that runs underneath: refining. Every serious unit or nanite income in the game passes through a refiner at some point, because refiners convert the cheap and abundant into the scarce and valuable while you are off doing something more interesting. It is the closest thing the game has to passive income, and the recipe list is deep enough that whole playthroughs are built around it. This guide covers the machine, the recipes that matter, and the loops worth automating.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For the broader unit economy, see [money making](/guides/money-making-guide/); for nanites specifically, [nanites farming](/guides/nanites-farming/).

## The Three Refiners

| Refiner | Power | Inputs | Role |
|---------|-------|--------|------|
| Portable | Carbon fuel slot | 1 input → 1 output | The field tool — deploy anywhere |
| Medium | Base power | 2 input slots | Combination recipes at your base |
| Large | Base power | 3 input slots | Factory throughput |

The portable refiner is a backpack appliance: craft it from modest materials, drop it on any surface, feed it carbon, and it works through your input while you mine the next deposit. Medium and large refiners are base infrastructure — they need a powered base ([the electrical guide](/base-building/electrical-wiring-guide/) covers that side) — and their extra input slots are what unlock the real recipe book, because the best conversions combine two materials into something better than either.

Two mechanical notes worth knowing before the recipes. First, refiners have **input capacity limits per slot** — load them in batches and top up as they consume. Second, refiners **pause, not fail**: out of fuel or power they simply stop, contents intact, and resume when fed. Refining is forgiving by design.

## The Recipe Families That Matter

### Chromatic Metal: The Backbone

The game's central refinement: **copper-family ores into chromatic metal** — the material half of all technology, including the [Base Computer](/base-building/base-computer-placement/) itself. Cadmium, emeril, and indium refine into chromatic metal at increasingly generous yields, with the activated variants richest of all. Every long session chews through chromatic metal, and every veteran keeps a refiner working ore into it as a background process.

### Glass: The Craft Chain Starter

**Frost crystal refines into glass**, and glass feeds the advanced crafting chains — the crafted-goods economy starts at this conversion. Frost crystal grows abundantly on cold worlds and in cultivation, which is why so many farm bases are cold-weather bases: the [planet choice](/base-building/best-base-planets/) and the recipe list are the same decision.

### Precious Metals Down the Chain

Silver, gold, and platinum refine **downward into each other** — gold into silver, silver onward — the standard way to convert a mining glut of one metal into the specific metal a craft recipe wants. It is the exchange desk of the periodic table: not a profit engine, but the tool that turns "I have the wrong precious metal" into "I have the right one."

### Gases Into Components

The atmospheric gases — nitrogen, sulphurine, radon, harvested with collectors or gathered from the right planets — combine in refiners into the components that advanced crafting consumes. This is where refining stops being a side activity and becomes a factory: gas collectors feeding refiners feeding crafters, all on one powered base.

### The Nanite Loop: Runaway Mould

**Runaway mould refines into nanite clusters** — slowly, at a fixed ratio, but unattended. The mould grows in patches on corrupted and dissonant planets (the same loop that produces [sentinel multitools](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/)), and the play is logistical: drop a medium refiner near a patch, load the harvest, and the nanites accrue while you do anything else. It is not the fastest nanite income — the [nanites guide](/guides/nanites-farming/) ranks the methods — but it is the most passive.

### Fuel and Utility Conversions

The small stuff that smooths every session: **carbon refines into condensed carbon** for denser fuel; oxygen and carbon combine into the fuels your life support and engines actually want; dioxite and other processed elements refine from their raw components when a station trader is out of stock. Utility conversions rarely make money — they save trips.

## Turning Recipes Into Units

Refining alone is a decent income. Refining **feeding crafting** is the real one: raw materials → refined components → crafted trade goods, each step multiplying value. The classic loops all follow the shape:

1. **Chromatic-metal chains** — mine copper-family ore, refine to chromatic metal, craft upward into technology and goods.
2. **Glass chains** — grow frost crystal, refine to glass, craft the advanced goods that consume it.
3. **Chlorine expansion** — the old standby: chlorine plus oxygen refining into more chlorine, a pure multiplication loop for unit printing.

The consistent theme: never sell what a refiner can upgrade first. Raw ore sells as raw ore forever; the same ore walked two steps down a recipe chain sells as technology. The full strategy, with the farm-base setups that feed it, is in [money making](/guides/money-making-guide/).

## Building the Refining Base

The refinery base has a shape, and it is the same for everyone:

- **A powered room** — medium and large refiners on the grid ([power guide](/base-building/electrical-wiring-guide/)).
- **Storage adjacency** — inputs and outputs beside the machines, in the [numbered containers](/base-building/storage-containers/): ores in one number, refined goods in the next.
- **A teleporter at the door** — the base is a stop on your loop, in and out in minutes.
- **Optional: cultivation** — frost crystal and other growable inputs within walking distance of the refiners.

Three large refiners, a teleporter, and a full storage wall is the entire factory, and it fits in one cuboid room. The returns compound for the rest of the save.

## Rules of Thumb

- **Never idle a refiner at your base** — power is free once the grid is up; an empty refiner is an unused income.
- **Refine before selling, always** — the two-step rule that separates rich saves from busy ones.
- **Batch, top up, collect** — respect the slot limits; top up inputs as they drain rather than babysitting.
- **Collect before you log off** — refiners pause safely, but the habit removes the risk entirely.
- **Portables are for the field** — anything worth doing twice belongs on base power in a medium or large.

Refining is No Man's Sky's quiet answer to "how do I afford all of this": a system that works while you don't. Set up the room once, keep the machines fed, and the unit and nanite problems quietly solve themselves in the background of every expedition.
