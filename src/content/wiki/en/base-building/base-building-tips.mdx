Every No Man's Sky player builds the same first base: a lopsided timber box on a hillside, door facing the wind, power line sagging across the window. Then, a few weeks later, they build something with a floating walkway and a two-story aquarium and wonder what changed. The answer is never playtime — it is technique. The builder's toolset has a dozen small disciplines that compound, and nobody tells you any of them. This guide is the telling.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For the fundamentals — computer, first room, starter power — read the [base building guide](/base-building/base-building-guide/) first; this page assumes you can already raise a box.

## Camera and Placement Discipline

1. **Free camera is half the builder.** While holding a part, detach the camera and orbit the build. Ceilings, roof sockets, and high wall mounts become point-and-click instead of jetpack archaeology.
2. **Place, adjust, confirm.** Almost every part can be rotated, tilted, or nudged after placement-preview and before commit. The reflex to slow down for one second is what separates clean builds from crooked ones.
3. **Snap, do not eyeball.** Parts have sockets — walls to floors, roofs to walls, corridors to room mouths. Snapped parts auto-align; eyeballed parts drift a few degrees each time, and by the tenth piece your cathedral is a funhouse.
4. **Delete is cheap, redo is not.** Deleting refunds part of the materials. When a piece fights you, remove it and re-snap instead of stacking a second piece to hide the first.

## Structure: Choose Your Skeleton

5. **Cuboid rooms are the workhorse.** They tile in any direction, accept doors and windows on every face, and combine into any floor plan. Round prefabs are beautiful but geometrically bossy — use them as accents, cuboids as bones.
6. **Foundations before walls.** A flat foundation pad — timber, metal, or foundation pieces — makes everything above it square. Build on raw terrain and every joint inherits the slope.
7. **Modular beats monumental.** Design in repeatable sections: a room module, a corridor, a stair core. Copy the module six times and you have a building; design one monolith and every change is surgery.
8. **Go vertical early.** Vertical builds cost less foundation, dodge terrain problems almost entirely, and look dramatically more ambitious. A stair core with stacked cuboid rooms is the cheapest "wow" in the game.

## Terrain: The Tool You Forgot You Own

9. **The Terrain Manipulator is a building tool.** Level pads, cut foundation trenches, bury support pillars, carve hillside entrances. Flattening for thirty seconds before building saves thirty minutes of crooked walls.
10. **Bury the ugly parts.** Pumps, wires, and structural clutter disappear into terrain you add afterward — mound earth over the machinery and the build reads clean.
11. **Terrain edits are revertible.** The Base Computer keeps a restore point that can roll back the terrain you edited — the escape hatch when a dig goes wrong, detailed in [Base Computer placement](/base-building/base-computer-placement/).

## Power and Utilities

12. **Route power like a builder, not an electrician.** Power lines snap to parts and can be re-anchored and rerouted; run them along ceilings and skirting lines, through walls, under floors. A mess of sagging cables is the number one visual tell of a young base.
13. **One grid, many sources.** Generators, batteries, and consumers all join a single base grid — you do not wire room-by-room. The full generator ladder is in the [electrical wiring guide](/base-building/electrical-wiring-guide/).
14. **Hide switches in the workflow.** Wall switches and proximity sensors automate doors and lighting; a proximity sensor on the entrance light means you never think about the entrance light again.

## Storage and Function

15. **Storage containers share by number.** Build the same container number at different bases — including freighter rooms — and they access the same inventory. The system is covered in [storage containers](/base-building/storage-containers/).
16. **Function first, decoration forever.** A base that does something — refines, farms, stores, teleports — justifies returning to it, and only bases you return to ever get finished.

## The Habit Layer

17. **Scout before you commit.** Circle the site on foot, check sightlines, weather, and [whether the planet deserves you](/base-building/best-base-planets/). The computer cannot be moved, only deleted.
18. **Build the skeleton, light it, then decorate.** Lighting placed before decoration shapes the room; lighting placed after just polishes mistakes.
19. **Limit your palette.** Two or three materials per build. Every palette you own, used everywhere, reads as noise — restraint reads as design.
20. **Finish the box before starting the cathedral.** The object limit is real, per-base, and closer than it looks. A completed small build teaches more than an abandoned big one — and [parts unlock](/base-building/base-parts-unlock/) steadily widens what your "small" can be.

## What the Top Builders Actually Do Differently

They prototype. A test pillar of five cuboid frames in the yard answers "will this window wall work" for the cost of a minute's materials. They build sections on flat foundations near their teleporter, then replicate the pattern at the real site. And they steal shamelessly — visiting other players' bases through the portal network is the game's best architecture school, and half the techniques above started in someone else's starport.

Your second base will be better than your first. Your tenth will not resemble your first. That progression — not any single trick — is the real tip.
