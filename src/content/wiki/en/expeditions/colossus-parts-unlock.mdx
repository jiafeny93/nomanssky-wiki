The Colossus spent years as No Man's Sky's least-loved vehicle: too slow to race, too bulky to explore in, and outclassed on foot. The Remnant update in February 2026 rewrote its job description entirely — waste hauling, salvage processing, and a full visual customization system with roughly thirteen new parts. The catch, and the reason so many players are searching for how Colossus parts unlock, is that those parts are split across three different systems. The vehicle itself is a permanent unlock at the Space Anomaly, most of the Remnant parts arrive through scrap salvaging, and the three best-looking pieces were Expedition 21 rewards. **Last updated: August 18, 2026.**

## What Is the Colossus?

The Colossus is the largest of the game's six Exocraft: an armoured, six-wheeled hauler introduced in the Pathfinder update in March 2017. Where the Nomad glides and the Pilgrim races, the Colossus lumbers — it has always been the slowest craft in the garage, and historically the clumsiest, prone to snagging on terrain that smaller vehicles shrugged off.

What it has always had going for it is capacity. The Colossus carries the biggest cargo hold of any Exocraft and ships with an Exocraft Mining Laser pre-installed, which made it the default bulk-hauling choice long before anyone called it fun. The Remnant update leaned hard into exactly that identity. Colossus handling was reworked — braking downhill and first-person turning both got named fixes in the patch notes — obstacle collisions were made far less punishing, and a whole scrap economy was bolted around the vehicle: industrial waste to collect, Waste Processing Plants to deliver it to, and a tippable flatbed to move it in bulk.

In short, the Colossus is no longer the vehicle you apologize for driving, and its parts system is now genuinely part of playing the game well.

## Step 1: Unlock the Colossus Geobay (the Vehicle Itself)

Before any parts question, there is the vehicle unlock, and the 2026 answer is far simpler than the one veterans remember.

1. **Farm Salvaged Data.** Land on any planet, equip the Analysis Visor, and scan for Buried Technology Module markers. Dig each one up with the Terrain Manipulator; every module yields a few Salvaged Data. You need ten for this step, which takes most players well under an hour on a data-rich world.
2. **Buy the blueprint at the Space Anomaly.** Summon the Anomaly, find the Construction Research station, and purchase the Colossus Geobay blueprint for 10 Salvaged Data.
3. **Build the Geobay.** The bay is a base part you can place anywhere on a planet's surface, inside or outside a claimed base. Per the community wiki the build cost is a Metal Plating, a Voltaic Cell, and a Dynamic Resonator — cheap for a vehicle. Constructing the bay spawns your first Colossus.

The prerequisite question comes up constantly, so here it is plainly: **you do not need any other Exocraft bay first.** Geobay blueprints are independent of one another at the research station, and the Colossus can be your first and only Exocraft — exactly what players doing pure salvage runs on a starting planet do. Older guides describe a freighter-based Exocraft Technician quest line; that path was replaced by the Anomaly research station long ago, so do not hire freighter crew just for this.

Two quality-of-life follow-ups. Research the **Orbital Exocraft Materializer** when you can afford it: with that built, you can summon any Exocraft you own anywhere on a planet, so the Geobay becomes a garage and customization shop rather than something you rebuild at every base. And exocraft upgrade modules — engines, boosters, weapons, mining laser improvements — are bought separately with nanites from the technology vendors aboard the Anomaly.

## Step 2: Remnant Update Parts Every Save Can Unlock

The Remnant update's Colossus customization system is permanent content, not expedition content. The patch notes describe cabins, chassis attachments, running gear, and paint finishes, with each cabin bringing its own cockpit interior and the Geobay storing up to six named configurations you can swap between at will. How each part actually arrives varies:

| Part | What it does | How it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cabin alternatives | New cabs, each with a matching cockpit interior | Remnant update general pool |
| Off-road running gear | Superelastic wheels, tracked treads, off-road setups | Remnant update general pool |
| Paint finishes | Finishes that layer over your chosen colour | General pool, plus one expedition exclusive |
| Flatbed module | Tippable bed with a containment field for waste hauls | Waste-salvaging gameplay |
| Exocraft-Mounted Furnace | Processes industrial waste while you drive | Waste-salvaging gameplay |
| Excavation Blade | Harvests resources from obstacles destroyed by collision | Colossus tech added by the update |
| Cyclops Scope | Lets you use the Analysis Visor from the driver's seat | Exocraft tech added by the update |
| Trucking title modules | Unique customization modules tied to achievements | Earn the new scrap-hauling player titles |

The reliable route through that list is the waste-salvaging loop the update built: collect industrial waste with the Gravitino Coil (available at the Space Anomaly or at Waste Processing Plants), sell it at a plant, and work through the new trucking titles. The patch notes are explicit that each of those title achievements unlocks a Colossus customization module across all your saves. The exact vendor for every individual utility module is not itemized in the official notes, so treat the customization screen at your Geobay — which shows owned versus locked parts — as the in-game source of truth.

## Step 3: The Three Expedition-Exclusive Parts

Expedition 21: Remnant ran for roughly six weeks from mid-February 2026 and was built entirely around the Colossus: a single-planet salvage run that funnels you into a hauler during the first phase. Its reward pool contained exactly three exclusive Colossus modules, per the official patch notes:

- **Colossus Mechanical Legs** — an arachnid-style leg set that replaces the wheels and completely changes the vehicle's silhouette. The headline item of the entire update.
- **Asymmetrical cabin** — the one cab option that never entered the general pool.
- **Camouflage finish** — a limited-edition paint finish.

Community guides from the original run place the Mechanical Legs in Phase 4 of the expedition's five phases. Around the Colossus gear, the run also paid out posters, decals, titles, the Toot Toot trucking-horn gesture, and the five-piece Heirloom armour set. ## Missed Expedition 21? Your Options in 2026

You have two, and the first is better than most players expect.

**Option 1: Replay it from the expedition terminal.** Past expeditions, Remnant included, stay playable from the expedition terminal aboard the Space Anomaly. Start the run, complete the milestones, and the exclusive Colossus modules are yours — expedition rewards are account-wide unlocks that you redeem on any save through the Quicksilver Synthesis Companion. The Mechanical Legs are not currently missable; they cost an evening of driving, not a miracle. The same replay system is how players are catching up on every older run, and our general [expedition guide](/expeditions/expedition-guide) covers the milestone mechanics if Remnant is your first.

**Option 2: Wait for a Redux.** Hello Games periodically re-runs entire expeditions as limited-time Redux events — five of them back to back between November 2025 and January 2026. A Redux mainly adds community buzz and the occasional bonus item; the reward track itself you can already earn through replay. ## Is the Colossus Actually Worth Using?

An honest appraisal, three jobs at a time:

- **Hauling: excellent, and now purpose-built.** The largest cargo hold of any Exocraft, plus the flatbed's containment field and the mounted furnace, turns the Colossus into a mobile recycling rig. Post-Remnant, waste runs pay out materials and rewards at the processing plants, so the hauler finally has a loop that pays for its own fuel.
- **Mining: serviceable, not optimal.** The stock Exocraft Mining Laser clears deposits from the driver's seat, upgrade modules sharpen it, and the Excavation Blade quietly harvests from everything you drive through, while the Cyclops Scope lets you scan without stopping. On-foot multitool mining is still faster for most deposits; the Colossus wins when the job is bulk collection near a base.
- **Terrain: much improved, with one caveat.** The Remnant handling fixes, off-road wheels, and tracked treads fixed most of the historic clunkiness. The caveat is the Mechanical Legs themselves: community reports from the original expedition say they come to a dead stop against small single resource plants such as Di-Hydrogen patches. That behavior is community-documented rather than patch-note confirmed, so test the legs before you commit to the spider look full time.

The verdict: as a pure explorer vehicle the Roamer and Nomad still win, but for base-building supply runs, scrap economics, and sheer presence, the Colossus has never been in better shape — and it is the only Exocraft with a customization system this deep.
