Every few months the search trends light up with it: *No Man's Sky 2*. The question feels reasonable — most games this successful get a sequel, and No Man's Sky has now been successful for a decade. But the honest answer is short, and this article gives it to you straight before spending your click: **No Man's Sky 2 has never been announced, no credible evidence says one exists, and everything Hello Games has done for ten years says their plan is the opposite of making one.** What exists instead is arguably more interesting — and if what you actually want is a new Hello Games game, that *does* exist, and it is called Light No Fire.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026.** Everything below is sourced from Hello Games' own announcements and verifiable public record — no rumors dressed as leaks.

## The Direct Answer

As of August 2026:

- **No sequel has ever been announced, teased, or trademarked** in any credible form.
- Hello Games' most recent major statement of intent is the **Cosmos update** — a tenth-anniversary overhaul of the original game, announced August 2026 and rolling out in stages ([what's in it](/cosmos-update/cosmos-new-features/)).
- The studio's only announced future game is **Light No Fire**, a new IP — not a No Man's Sky sequel.

A studio celebrating its game's tenth anniversary by shipping the biggest update in the series' history is not a studio pivoting to a numbered sequel. That is the entire argument, and the last ten years back it completely.

## The Real Sequel Has Been Shipping for Ten Years

Here is the pattern that makes *No Man's Sky 2* the wrong question. Since the 2016 launch, Hello Games has repeatedly rebuilt the game's fundamentals *inside* the game, free:

| What a sequel would have done | What Hello Games did instead |
|-------------------------------|------------------------------|
| New engine, new rendering | Free visual overhauls, culminating in the Worlds updates' planet, water, and creature rework |
| A second game's worth of content | Dozens of major updates: base building, multiplayer, freighters, living ships, expeditions |
| Paid expansion or season pass | All of it free, across ten years |
| Fresh player base, new purchase | One save, one purchase, carried forward across console generations |

The 2026 content alone — [Swarm](/guides/swarm-battles-guide/) in May, [Xeno Arena](/guides/pet-battling-guide/) in April, [Cosmos](/cosmos-update/cosmos-update-guide/) in August — is more new gameplay than many full sequels ship with. The commercial logic is the studio's moat: every update deepens the one game everyone already owns, and no sequel could compete with ten years of accumulated systems without abandoning them.

## Light No Fire: The Actual Next Hello Games Game

If your search for *No Man's Sky 2* is really a search for *the next Hello Games game* — that exists:

- **What it is:** a fantasy adventure about exploration, building, survival, and role-playing-depth adventure, set on **a fantasy planet the size of Earth**, playable cooperatively.
- **Announced:** The Game Awards, December 2023.
- **Release date:** none announced — the Steam page lists it as *to be announced*, no public beta or playtest exists, and outside estimates place it beyond 2026. Treat any concrete date you read anywhere as invented.
- **The connection to No Man's Sky:** shared studio, shared procedural ambitions — a whole planet instead of a whole galaxy. The community's working theory is that the big No Man's Sky technical updates doubled as proving grounds for it; that is informed speculation, not confirmation.

That is the entire verifiable record. When there is news, it will come from the official site and Steam page first.
