Somewhere around your third hour in No Man's Sky, you will warp into a system and find a freighter under pirate attack. Win that fight and the captain will hand you the entire ship. That single encounter is the hinge of mid-game progression: a freighter is a mobile base, a warehouse, a fleet command center, and eventually a second home, and the game gives you your first one for free. The only decisions that matter are when to take it and which one to take, because you only get the free ride while you own none.

**Last updated: August 27, 2026.** Freighter mechanics have been stable since the 2022 Endurance rework, with additions through 2025's Voyagers update and the staged Cosmos rollout this month. Everything below reflects the game as it plays today, not as it played when most of the top-ranking guides on this topic were written in 2018; current-year changes are tracked in our [2026 update guide](/guides/new-update-2026).

## What a Freighter Actually Gives You

A freighter is not a ship you fly. It hangs in space like a station, is summoned through the quick menu, and receives you through a blue-lit entrance. You own **one at a time**; buying a replacement trades the old one in at roughly two-thirds of its value, and your interior base migrates with you. In exchange for those limits you get the closest thing No Man's Sky has to a capital ship:

- **A mobile base.** Capital freighters give a 21x21 buildable footprint over multiple floors. Storage rooms built aboard sync with storage containers 0-9, a Teleport Chamber makes the ship a teleport destination, and an Orbital Exocraft Materialiser summons your exocraft anywhere in the system.
- **Remote inventory.** Install a **Matter Beam** and you can transfer items to and from the freighter from anywhere, which quietly doubles your effective cargo capacity.
- **A frigate fleet.** Own up to **30 frigates** in five specialties (combat, exploration, industrial, trade, support) and send them on timed expeditions for units and rare loot from Fleet Command Rooms.
- **Long legs.** Freighter hyperdrive technology pushes warp range past what most starships manage, and the ship carries you between systems in one jump.
- **Quality-of-life rooms.** A Galactic Trade Room for trading without docking, and the newer **Scanner Room** and **Stellar Extractor Room**, which scan every planet in a system at once and passively harvest materials based on the star's class.
- **A squadron.** The Manage Fleet terminal on the bridge also recruits up to four NPC wingmen who follow you into pirate fights.

Once you own one freighter, pirate dreadnought battles start appearing as well, and those can be won for salvage or, since the Omega update, claimed as ships in their own right. The full capture loop — forcing the surrender, boarding, and choosing between seizing the flagship and taking tribute — has its own [pirate freighter capture guide](/guides/pirate-freighter-capture/).

## How the Free Rescue Battle Triggers

The free freighter is not random luck. It is a scripted encounter with precise conditions.

| Condition | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Playtime | Roughly **3 hours** of accumulated play since your last rescue battle |
| Warps | **5 warp jumps** after the last battle; the fight starts on the warp that completes both conditions |
| Location | Inhabited systems only. No uncharted systems, no systems with a black hole, no systems with an Atlas Interface |
| Difficulty | Space combat must not be set to None in the difficulty settings |

When it fires, you warp in on a freighter already under attack by **5 or 8 pirates**. Destroy all of them (local militia will assist if your standing with the system's race is good), and the captain hails you, invites you to land, and offers the freighter. If you do not own a freighter, the offer is **free**. You can also choose a payment reward instead and fly off.

Between battles, the same 3-hour-plus-5-warps cycle runs again from zero, which is what makes freighters farmable rather than lucky.

## The Decline Trick: Why Veterans Say No to the First Offer

The single most useful piece of freighter knowledge is this: **the offer stays free until you claim one.** Declining does not burn your free freighter. And the game's internal rules make early offers deliberately weak:

1. **Your first two rescues are always system freighters**, the smaller hulls capped at **19 cargo slots**.
2. **From the third rescue onward, every offer is a capital freighter**, the "Star Destroyer"-class hulls with 24-34 starting cargo slots and the full-size interior.
3. Class is rolled fresh each battle, so holding out also gives you more chances at A-class and S-class hulls, which matter because **freighter class can never be upgraded**.

Put together: a player who declines the first two offers and claims the third gets a bigger, better ship for exactly the same price, which is nothing. The community shorthand, "decline the first freighter," is really "defer your claim until the rolls are worth claiming."

Be honest with yourself about the cost before committing to the purist route. Every declined offer means another full 3-hour, 5-warp cycle before the next one, and the first two rescues come naturally during normal play, so refusing them is mostly a decision to wait a few more hours, not a grind. The risk is inverted, too: if you genuinely need storage, Matter Beam logistics, or frigate income *right now*, take the free C-class. Slots expand with Cargo Bulkheads, the interior is the same size on every rescue until capital hulls unlock, and nothing about accepting blocks you from trading up later. A free freighter today beats a perfect freighter next week if you are drowning in cargo.

One mechanical note: when you decline, choosing the payment option is fine and keeps future offers free. Only actually accepting the ship flips you into ownership, after which rescue captains sell rather than gift.

## Trigger Rules, Odds, and the Farming Loop

Once you understand the cycle, the loop for hunting something specific is short.

- **Every system owns exactly one capital freighter model.** The hull you see in a given system is always the same one; only its class and slot roll change from battle to battle. Find a model you love and you can farm that system until the class cooperates.
- **Economy shapes the class roll.** Community testing puts S-class freighter odds around **2% in wealthy three-star economies** and **5% in outlaw systems**, with wealthy systems also feeding far more A-class rolls. Outlaw capitals can arrive with odd slot counts, so most hunters stick to opulent economies.
- **The reload trick still works.** If a battle is already in progress when you warp in, it is scripted. Reload your save and the next jump is guaranteed to produce another battle, letting you re-roll a system's class repeatedly, or chain between systems.
- **Wealthy systems are findable.** An Economy Scanner on your starship, or the freighter's Interstellar Scanner, labels economies before you commit to the warp.

That loop, tuned for maximum efficiency, is its own topic: our [capital freighter farming guide](/starships/capital-freighter-farming) covers the save-reload pattern, the best economy tiers, and how to shorten the 3-hour cooldown honestly.

## Capital vs System Freighters

The two hull classes you will be choosing between:

| | System freighter | Capital freighter |
|---|------------------|-------------------|
| Starting cargo slots | 15-19 | 24-34 |
| Starting tech slots | 8-12 | 12-20 |
| Purchase price range | 5-23 million units | 26-180 million units |
| Buildable floors | 15 (19x21) | 14 (21x21) |

Slots grow with **Cargo Bulkheads**, and the expansion ceiling is set by class: cargo caps at **48** (C), **72** (B), **100** (A), and **120** (S), with tech caps at 27/30/50/60. Because the ceiling moves so sharply with class, an A-class or S-class hull eventually outgrows a C-class one by dozens of slots, which is the entire argument for declining early offers.

## Your First Week Aboard: What to Unlock First

Freighters start hollow. Prioritize in roughly this order.

1. **Research the Matter Beam** at the bridge Upgrade Control terminal. It costs Salvaged Frigate Modules, which come from crashed freighters, derelict freighters, Nexus missions, and frigate expeditions, and it converts the ship from a destination into infrastructure you can use from any planet.
2. **Buy the freighter hyperdrive chain.** The Warp Core Resonator, Plasmatic Warp Injector, and Reality De-threader stack hundreds of light-years of range; the shielding line unlocks red, green, and blue systems.
3. **Build Fleet Command Rooms** and unlock frigate fuel blueprints at their terminals, then spend your first million units on frigates. Fleet income funds everything else on this list.
4. **Build storage rooms**, which sync with your planetary containers and free your exosuit from hoarding.
5. **Add Cargo Bulkheads** as they arrive from rescue rewards and derelicts, and install **freighter upgrade modules** looted from derelict freighters for hyperdrive range and fleet stat bonuses.
6. **Recolor the hull** at the Upgrade Control, and keep an eye out for a **Dream Aerial** on frigate expeditions. It starts the chain to Organic Frigates, the companion grind to the [Living Ship quest](/starships/living-ship-guide).

When you eventually trade up to a better hull, know the migration quirk: your base is supposed to transfer automatically, but the reliable way to trigger the transfer prompt is to leave a single item in the old freighter's cargo before accepting the new one.

## Freighters in 2026: What Has Changed Recently

Most guides ranking for "how to get a freighter" were written before the Endurance rework rebuilt freighter interiors, before Echoes and Omega made dreadnoughts claimable, and before 2025's Voyagers update added the Scanner Room and Stellar Extractor Room. None of that changed the free-rescue path: it is still three hours, five warps, and a captain's gratitude. What has changed is what the ship can do after you own it, and more may be coming, with Cosmos patch notes still rolling out and heat-resistant gear making star landings plausible. A stocked freighter parked in the system you are exploring is the natural resupply point for that kind of expedition, per our [Cosmos feature breakdown](/cosmos-update/cosmos-new-features).
