Everything in No Man's Sky begins with pointing a beam at a rock. The first hour of the game is mining; so, functionally, is the thousandth — activated indium farms, crashed freighter salvage, storm-glass runs all route back through the mining beam. Which makes the beam's upgrade path the first build decision every player makes and one of the most under-optimized in the game: a properly built beam strips deposits at several times the speed of a stock one and barely pauses to vent heat.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. This guide covers the full beam build: modules, the Advanced Mining Laser, heat, slots, and where the whole system's rules come from (see [multitool upgrades](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/) for the general system).

## What the Mining Beam Actually Is

The beam is the multitool's default core technology: a continuous laser that mines mineral deposits, strips flora for carbon, salvages derelict components, and damages enemies weakly, with no ammunition ever. Its performance rests on four knobs, all upgradeable:

| Knob | What it controls | How to raise it |
|------|------------------|-----------------|
| Beam intensity | Damage and mining speed per second | Mining Beam Upgrade modules |
| Heat capacity | How long before forced venting | Upgrade modules + heat tech |
| Cooling rate | How fast heat clears | Upgrade modules + heat tech |
| Mining range | Distance you can work from | Beam focus modules |

A stock beam is slow and overheats mid-deposit. A built beam chews through an outcrop before the heat bar matters. The difference is entirely the module layer.

## The Module Build, Step by Step

1. **Confirm the core.** The Mining Beam core technology installs by default on every tool. If you somehow removed it, reinstall before anything else.
2. **Buy three Mining Beam Upgrades, class S ideally.** Technology merchants on station concourses sell them for nanites, stock varying per system — wealthy three-star economies carry better classes, exactly as with [S-class ships](/starships/s-class-ships/). The Space Anomaly's merchant is the fallback. Bring [nanites](/guides/nanites-farming/); S-class stock is not cheap.
3. **Cluster for adjacency.** The three modules go in slots touching the Mining Beam core and each other — same-family grouping is a live stat bonus, visible the moment you move a module. Scattered modules are the most common silent nerf in the game.
4. **Supercharge the strongest.** If your tool's supercharged slots aren't spoken for by a weapon or scanner identity module, the strongest beam upgrade belongs in one. (On a dedicated mining tool, the beam module *is* the identity — supercharge it without hesitation.)
5. **Add heat tech if the loop still stalls.** Beam heat-management technologies complement the modules; install when the bar still fills mid-deposit after the module layer is done.

The three-module limit is hard — a fourth same-family module adds nothing. Depth comes from class and placement, not count.

## The Advanced Mining Laser: The Unofficial Upgrade Zero

The Advanced Mining Laser is a core technology rather than an upgrade module, and no beam build is finished without it: the base beam cannot complete mining on many high-value targets, while the advanced laser can. The recurring cases:

- **Tough ore outcrops** that a stock beam ticks to a stop on
- **Crashed freighter salvage**, where the valuable componentry needs the advanced laser to strip
- **Certain quest and dissonance-loop objectives** that check for the tech

Acquire it through its blueprint in the normal research flow, craft it, and install it near the beam cluster. It is the single highest-leverage "upgrade" in the beam line and the most common thing new players are missing when they ask why their beam can't finish a deposit.

## Mining as a Weapon (Briefly)

The beam never runs out of ammunition, which makes it the infinite-ammo fallback when a fight outlasts your boltcaster rounds — genuinely useful against the last drone of a wave. Upgraded beam damage is respectable; unupgraded, treat it as a last resort. Real combat builds are covered in [weapon modes](/multitools/weapon-modes-guide/); the beam's job on a combat tool is still the mining between fights.

## The Dedicated Mining Tool

Players who industrialize their mining — farm feedstock, refiner chains, [money routes](/guides/money-making-guide/) — eventually carry a tool where the beam is the identity:

| Grid region | Contents |
|-------------|----------|
| Supercharged slots | Strongest Mining Beam Upgrade |
| Technology block | Mining Beam core + Advanced Mining Laser + remaining beam upgrades, clustered |
| Second family | Scanner line (mining tools scan deposits too) or a light weapon line for hostile runs |
| General slots | Utility overflow |

Any tool body carries this build acceptably — a standard rifle is the traditional chassis, and no [royal](/multitools/royal-multitool/) or experimental is required for a beam that already eats deposits whole. The exotic bodies matter when you want one tool to do everything; a second, dedicated mining tool is the cheaper answer.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping the Advanced Mining Laser** and blaming the modules for stalled deposits.
- **Buying C or B class modules** and never replacing them — placeholder gear, fine for a day.
- **Scattering modules** and losing adjacency — check the grid the next time you play.
- **Supercharging a utility** while the beam module sits in a normal slot.
- **Fourth same-family module** — pure waste; the limit is three.

Build it once, correctly, and the game's most repeated action gets permanently faster.
