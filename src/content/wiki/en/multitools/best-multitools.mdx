Ask ten No Man's Sky players for the best multitool and you will get ten answers, because the honest answer depends on what you point the thing at. The multitool is every profession at once — mining laser, scanner, rifle, shotgun, grenade launcher — and the game deliberately spreads those talents across different tool types instead of concentrating them in one. A tool that doubles your scan income is not the tool that melts Sentinel quads.

This guide ranks every multitool type in the game — experimental, alien, royal, sentinel, the Autophage staff, and the standard pistol and rifle bodies — by what each is actually best at, where it spawns, and who should carry it. **Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50 and the Cosmos anniversary update; for how the underlying upgrade systems work, see our [multitool upgrades guide](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/).

## How These Rankings Work

Three factors drive every placement: the type's core stat bias (damage, scanner, mining), its practical quirks (where it spawns, what modules it accepts), and how hard a good roll is to acquire. Class grade — C through S — matters as much as type: an S-class anything outperforms a C-class specialty tool. Wealthy three-star economies spawn the best grades, the same lesson that drives [S-class ship hunting](/starships/s-class-ships/).

## Experimental: The Scanner King

Experimental multitools are what you buy to make money. They roll the strongest **scanner bonuses** in the game, which multiply the units every flora, fauna, and mineral scan pays out — a fully upgraded experimental can earn hundreds of thousands of units from a single fresh planet's scan streak. Their other stats are solid rather than spectacular, which makes them balanced general-purpose tools with a money specialty.

They are also rare. Experimentals appear occasionally at space stations and minor settlements in ordinary economies, and hunting one is a patience game of system-hopping with save-reloads to reroll the first-wave tool. The black-and-orange body is one of the most recognizable designs in the game. If units are your bottleneck, this is the tool — pair it with our [money making guide](/guides/money-making-guide/).

## Alien: The Damage Ceiling

Alien multitools are the wild cards: their stat rolls vary enormously between individual tools, so a great alien outdamages every other type while a bad one is worse than a standard rifle. The payoff for inspecting a lot of them is the **highest damage potential in the multitool pool**, backed by respectable mining stats.

They spawn most reliably where the law isn't — pirate and outlaw systems, at outlaw stations and the settlements on their planets. Outlaw space is also where you find [sentinel interceptors](/starships/sentinel-interceptor-guide/)' dissonant cousins and other contraband, so an outlaw farming loop can combine an alien tool hunt with ship scraping. Combat pilots should make this their target; see our [weapon modes guide](/multitools/weapon-modes-guide/) for what to install on it.

## Royal: The Prestige All-Rounder

Royal multitools exist to be seen. Gold-trimmed, ornate, and genuinely rare, they are the closest thing the multitool pool has to an exotic ship — and like exotics, their stats are very good across the board without hitting any single ceiling. A royal is a fine scanner, a fine miner, and a fine fighter that looks unlike anything else on the station wall.

They appear sparingly at space stations in wealthy economies and occasionally through other spawn channels, and no reliable mass-farming method exists — which is the point. Buy one when you see one. Stat-chasers should keep expectations calibrated: an experimental out-scans it and a god-roll alien out-hits it. As a single do-everything tool with swagger, nothing beats it.

## Sentinel: The Dissonance Specialist

Sentinel multitools come from the dissonance systems introduced with the Worlds-era Sentinel rework: corrupted planets, harmonic camps, and the Echo Locators that lead you there. They are obtained through gameplay rather than purchase — see our [sentinel multitool guide](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/) for the full step-by-step — and they are the natural companion piece to a [Sentinel interceptor](/starships/sentinel-interceptor-guide/), completing a dissonance loadout.

Statwise they sit in the strong all-rounder band with a combat lean and a distinctive red-black aesthetic. Their real hook is thematic and mechanical synergy: players who farm dissonant systems for interceptor ships and dreadnought fights will already have everything needed to claim one.

## The Autophage Staff: The Odd One

The staff is less a multitool than an alternate weapon platform — a modular construct assembled from Autophage parts, acquired through their questline. Its stats depend on the components used, it handles differently from conventional tools, and it exists mostly outside the normal spawn economy. It is not the answer to "which tool is best," but it is a rewarding sidegrade for players deep in the Autophage story. Treat it as a collector's project with a functional payoff.

## Standard Pistol and Rifle: The Workhorses

The humble C-to-A-class pistols and rifles that fill station walls everywhere are not junk — they are the tools most players actually mine with for most of the game. Rifles lean toward combat stats and more technology slots; pistols are compact with leaner slot counts. Both accept the full upgrade catalogue, so an A-class rifle with a disciplined module layout outperforms a neglected S-class specialty tool. Early on, spend units on slot capacity and [nanites](/guides/nanites-farming/) on modules before you spend either on rarity.

## The Verdict, By Playstyle

| If you mainly... | Carry this | Why |
|------------------|------------|-----|
| Scan and sell | Experimental | Scanner bonuses multiply passive income |
| Fight pirates and Sentinels | Alien (good roll) | Highest damage ceiling in the pool |
| Want one tool for everything | Royal | Balanced stats, unmatched looks |
| Farm dissonance content | Sentinel | Synergy with interceptors and harmonic camps |
| Are starting out | Standard rifle | Cheap slots, accepts everything, easy to replace |
| Collect oddball gear | Autophage staff | Unique platform, quest-gated |

However you specialize, the multiplier that matters most is the upgrade layer — module class, adjacency, and supercharged slot placement. That system is the same for every tool type, and it is where most players leave real performance on the table.
