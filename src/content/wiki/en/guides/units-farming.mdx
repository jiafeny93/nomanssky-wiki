Ask a No Man's Sky player what they farm and you learn their whole save. Hour ten: scanning money. Hour fifty: chlorine in a refiner. Hour two hundred: a grid of extractors on a hell-planet nobody visits, quietly minting more units than they can spend. Unit farming in this game has a tech tree of its own — but the tree was pruned in the Waypoint update, when the old king took a nerf it never recovered from. This guide ranks the loops that matter in 2026 with honest numbers, from the first scan to the farm that still pays while you sleep.

## 1. The Scanner Loop: First Money, Still Good Money

The humblest loop and the best early one: install three S-class Scanner Upgrade modules on any multitool, and every creature, plant, and mineral scan pays sums that make the early game trivial. Completing a planet's full fauna set stacks a bonus on top.

Why it tops the early ranking: zero upkeep, works everywhere, and the [module discipline](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/) costs a station or two of shopping. A fresh traveler with an upgraded scanner can fund their first real purchases from one planet's wildlife. It never scales to megabase money — but it buys the tools that do.

## 2. Chlorine Expansion: The Active Printer

The classic multiplication loop: salt and oxygen refine into chlorine, and chlorine plus more oxygen refines into *more* chlorine. Inputs are cheap and abundant — oxygen harvests in fat red plants everywhere and refines from kelp sacs and cave fodder — and the loop needs only a bank of refiners on base power.

Its character is active income: the refiners do the multiplying while you feed them, and a stocked [refining base](/guides/refining-recipes/) turns an hour of oxygen gathering into a stack of chlorine worth selling in any three-star system. One caveat from the patch history: the buy-oxygen version of this loop is gone — Hello Games removed oxygen from most trade terminals and trimmed chlorine's value — so the modern loop farms its own oxygen. With a hotspot and a refiner bank, community players still report 10-50M units per hour: the best ratio of setup cost to return among the active loops.

## 3. Craft Chains: The Ceiling Raiser

Raw materials sell as raw materials forever; walked down a recipe chain they multiply at every step. The veteran shape: refiners converting ore and gas into components, crafters turning components into high-value trade goods, storage buffering the pipeline. The endgame chains — the Stasis Device and Living Glass family — require scattered blueprints and rare materials, and pay accordingly: about 15.6 million units per Stasis Device or Fusion Ignitor from a fully grown resource base.

The character is semi-passive and fiddly: the [refining guide](/guides/refining-recipes/) covers the machine side, but the chain's throughput lives on logistics — gas collectors, frost crystal cultivation, and a [storage hub](/base-building/storage-containers/) that keeps stages separated. Highest skill ceiling in the units game.

## 4. The Activated Indium Farm: The Retired King

Here is the verdict most guides won't tell you, because their guides stopped being updated in 2021: **the activated indium farm was nerfed, hard, and it is no longer the best money in the game — but it is still the best set-and-forget build you can make.**

The history, briefly. Before the Waypoint update, activated indium sold for 949 units per piece, and a serious extractor stack on a strong hotspot was a money printer without equal. Waypoint cut the sell price to 165 — roughly a sixth of its old value — and added diminishing returns to oversized extraction. Community-reported consequence: one well-known large farm that sold around 220 million units per harvest sells about 35 million of it now.

What that means in practice:

- **It still works.** The deposit never depletes, extraction is free after the build, and millions of passive units per collection cycle is more money than most players will spend.
- **It no longer rules.** A maxed [frigate fleet](/starships/freighter-guide/) out-earns it passively at 50-70M per day, and active methods — [interceptor scrapping, trade routes](/guides/money-making-guide/) — beat its hourly rate by an order of magnitude.
- **Build it for the right reason.** Build an indium farm when you want income that requires zero play — not because a 2021 guide called it the meta.

The build is still the whole cost: a survey for the [hotspot](/base-building/best-base-planets/), the power and extractor grid ([electrical guide](/base-building/electrical-wiring-guide/)), and the patience to wire it. After that it is a money faucet with no moving parts — just a smaller faucet than the legends say.

## 5. Frigates: The Passive King Now

If the crown moved, it moved here. A [freighter](/starships/freighter-guide/) with a full frigate fleet sends squads on expeditions while you play, and they come back with units and tradeables at a community-reported 50-70M per day once fully leveled. Setup is money and time rather than construction — buy decent frigates, run them constantly, staff out the fleet — and the income survives you not logging in for a week better than any refiner does.

Pair it with the craft chains above and the fleet's material bonuses feed the very recipes that pay 15.6M per item.

## 6. Expeditions and Events: The Burst

Time-limited expeditions hand out ships, multitools, cosmetics, and stacks of tradeable rewards at rates the permanent game never offers — a single [expedition run](/expeditions/expedition-guide/) can drop assets worth millions plus pure cash rewards. Nexus missions and weekend events fill the gaps between expeditions with steady unit payouts and the community-currency side income ([Quicksilver](/guides/quicksilver-farming/)).

Not a farm in the steady sense — a harvest calendar. But a returning player who rides expeditions hard banks more value in two weeks than a pure farm produces in a month.

## Choosing by Save Stage

- **First 10 hours:** scanner loop, plus the [starter tips](/guides/starter-tips/) economy habits. Buy suit slots.
- **Hours 10–50:** chlorine expansion and first craft chains; begin the base ([building guide](/base-building/base-building-guide/)) that later loops assume; buy your first freighter and start the fleet.
- **Hours 50+:** max the frigate fleet first, then build the indium farm if you want truly idle income — and let both fund [S-class ship hunting](/starships/s-class-ships/) and everything after.

The quiet truth: units stop mattering. A working income stream makes every price in the game a rounding error, and the game's real currencies become time and attention — which is the point at which you are free to play whatever you actually enjoy. The farm is not the goal; the farm is what buys the goal.
