The Living Ship is No Man's Sky's organic starship: a creature that looks like a cross between a whale, an insect, and a warp-capable hull, grown from an egg instead of bought from a pilot. It arrived in the February 2020 Living Ship update and has stayed one of the game's most coveted trophies ever since, partly because it cannot simply be purchased, and partly because the quest to hatch one is deliberately slow. The whole chain is gated behind real-time timers, so the moment you decide you want one is the moment you should start.

## What a Living Ship Actually Is

Before you spend a week of timers on one, know what you are getting. A Living Ship is not a reskin. It is a parallel technology tree with its own rules:

- **Always S-class.** A hatched Living Ship cannot roll C, B, or A. The stats are still random within the S-class band, but the floor is high.
- **A fixed 36+30 layout.** It hatches with 36 Storage Sac cargo slots and 30 Organ Chamber tech slots, expanded later with organic Spawning Sacs rather than Storage Augmentations.
- **Colored-system access for free.** It warps to red, green, and blue star systems without a Cadmium, Emeril, or Indium Drive. Purple systems still require an Atlantid Drive.
- **Cheap to run.** Its organs recharge on basics like oxygen, silver, and chromatic metal instead of launch fuel and warp cells, and a fully grown Neural Assembly eventually recharges itself.
- **Incompatible with metal.** Normal starship weapons, upgrade modules, and Storage Augmentations cannot be installed. It carries exactly two weapons (Spewing Vents and Grafted Eyes), and it cannot be scrapped, only traded.

Piloted only by you, never by NPCs, it makes an excellent long-range explorer and a poor dedicated fighter. For what it is worth, a self-feeding ship that never needs a space station is also a comfortable companion for the long hauls that [Cosmos's new features](/cosmos-update/cosmos-new-features) are opening up.

## Step 1: Buy the Void Egg

The quest does not start until you own a specific item, and that item has exactly one source.

1. Summon the **Space Anomaly** from anywhere in space and dock.
2. Find the **Quicksilver Synthesis Companion**, the vendor at the Quicksilver counter in the Anomaly's back area.
3. Buy the **Void Egg** for **3,200 Quicksilver**.
4. Keep it in your inventory as a plain "Void Egg." An "Active Void Egg" means the quest has already begun, which is fine, but the purchase itself is always the dormant egg.

Earning the Quicksilver is the real gate. Nexus missions on the Anomaly pay a few hundred Quicksilver per run, the weekend community mission pays a large chunk more, and a brand-new save can realistically fund the egg in about four days of casual mission play. Check whether the current or most recent expedition has a Void Egg milestone first, because [past expeditions have awarded eggs outright](/expeditions/expedition-rewards-list), which skips the entire grind.

One more prerequisite: the final quest phase requires portal glyphs, specifically the first **12 of the 16**. If you have not collected them, talk to Travellers on space stations, pay the 100 nanites for a grave location, and visit the graves to bank glyphs before the timers start. Waiting until the last phase to hunt glyphs is the classic mistake, because it stalls a quest that is already slow.

## Step 2: The Starbirth Quest, Phase by Phase

With the egg aboard, fly into space and **pulse**. Pulsing in any system eventually produces a space encounter with a strange alien starship, which activates the egg and starts **Starbirth**. Community testing says the encounter triggers fastest while pulsing in blue star systems. From there the quest runs through five organ phases, each following the same rhythm: a space encounter points you at a system, a Korvax Plaque on the surface asks for a crafted organ component, and the component you deliver matures on a real-time timer.

| # | Phase | Egg becomes | What you actually do | Timer after |
|---|-------|-------------|----------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Mature Neural Stem | Active Void Egg | Find the Korvax Plaque, craft a Consciousness Bridge | 18–25.5 hours |
| 2 | Mature Heart Node | Stirring Void Egg | Extreme barren planet, craft a Pulsating Core | 19–25 hours |
| 3 | Hardened Shell | Awakened Void Egg | Lush planet, craft an Impossible Membrane | 20–24 hours |
| 4 | Singularity Core | Cracking Void Egg | Boundary Failure site, craft Seeds of Glass | 18–26 hours |
| 5 | Soul Chamber | final egg state | Portal to a glyph address, collect three Traveller souls | none |

Details worth knowing before each phase, so nothing catches you mid-timer:

1. **Mature Neural Stem.** The plaque teaches you the Consciousness Bridge blueprint and marks a crashed freighter holding the **Hexite** you need. Community-documented quantities: **250 Hexite, 60 Pugneum, 1 Korvax Casing**. Pugneum comes from destroyed Sentinels if you do not have a stack.
2. **Mature Heart Node.** You receive the Pulsating Core blueprint plus a **Solar Ray** multitool attachment; use it on metal deposits to collect the **Liquid Sun** the core requires.
3. **Hardened Shell.** The Impossible Membrane needs a **Hypnotic Eye**, found at the marked underwater Abandoned Building, and **Living Water**, obtained by returning to space and interacting with a **Child of Helios** anomaly.
4. **Singularity Core.** At a Boundary Failure you get the Seeds of Glass blueprint and the **Animus Beam**, which harvests **Fragmented Qualia** from living creatures. Yes, you are gently zapping wildlife for parts; a lush planet with docile animals makes this fast.
5. **Soul Chamber.** No timer, but a puzzle. The egg recites a glyph poem, and you portal to the address it describes, take coordinates from an Abandoned Building terminal, then fill the chamber with **three souls** from Traveller Graves whose locations are hinted along the way. If you portaled in, you must leave through the same portal to progress.

The timers track the real clock, not hours played, so organs keep maturing while the game is closed. Each phase's objective marker uses the Target Sweep scanner, so you never need to read planetary coordinates by hand. Do not lose the quest-unique items (Hexite especially); the markers do not reappear.

## Step 3: Hatching at the Fallen Host

The ending is the one part of the quest where rushing costs you.

1. After the third soul and the return trip through the portal, **make a manual save** before entering your starship, because boarding triggers the hatch decision.
2. Board, receive the transmission, and let the egg hatch. A **Fallen Host** bio-ship is marked as a crash site in your current system.
3. Inspect it with **Photo Mode** from the quick menu instead of landing and overwriting your save. If the appearance disappoints you, reload the manual save, travel to a different system, and let the transmission fire again there, because each system rolls different ships.
4. When you are satisfied, land and perform **Biogenesis**, feeding the matured Brain, Heart, Shell, Core, and Soul back into the ship.
5. Claim it. The first Living Ship is **free**.

One documented bug to preempt: keep all five matured organs in your Exosuit, your active starship, or a freighter with a Matter Beam installed when you collect the Fallen Host. If the game cannot find the organs at the moment of hatching, a mechanical ship spawns instead, which is not what you spent a week growing.

## Growing and Upgrading Your Living Ship

The hatch is the beginning of the second grind, because organic ships upgrade through organic parts. The organ-to-tech mapping:

| Living organ | Replaces |
|--------------|----------|
| Neural Assembly | Launch Thrusters |
| Pulsing Heart | Pulse Engine |
| Singularity Cortex | Hyperdrive |
| Scream Suppressor | Deflector Shield |
| Spewing Vents | Photon Cannon |
| Grafted Eyes | Phase Beam |

**Psychonic Eggs** are the upgrade currency. They hatch into random organic upgrade modules for the organs above, and every module can be grown up to **S-class with nanites**, at which point some organs pick up bonus behavior, like a fully upgraded Neural Assembly that recharges itself. Spawning Sacs, dropped alongside them, add inventory slots. Place upgrades adjacent to their parent organ, up to three per inventory type, for the standard adjacency bonus.

Psychonic Eggs come from two sources. First, a fleet containing **Organic Frigates** returns them from expeditions; you attract your first organic frigate by crafting a **Dream Aerial**, itself a rare frigate-expedition return, which means a functioning freighter and fleet come first (our [freighter guide](/starships/freighter-guide) and [capital freighter farming guide](/starships/capital-freighter-farming) cover building that quickly). Second, the living-ship space encounter itself: when one appears while you pulse, shoot it and it drops eggs. Utility modules also drop this way, including the Wormhole Brain, a combined economy and conflict scanner.

## Rerolling Your Ship's Look

There is no cosmetic reroll for Living Ships as of this writing. The ship customization system added for metal starships does not apply to them, and an owned Living Ship's appearance is permanent. Your leverage is entirely at the hatch: save-scum the Fallen Host step as described above, or simply move to another system and let the ship generate there, since every system's pool differs. Hunters chasing a specific look share portal coordinates for documented ships in the community catalogue, and you can steer a fresh hatch toward those planets before accepting the signal.

## Is the Living Ship Worth It?

As a pure stat stick, a great fighter or exotic still competes with it, and the weapon limitation is real. But nothing else in the game is guaranteed S-class, free, self-recharging, and capable of jumping into colored systems with zero drive installation. Start the egg the day you decide you want one, play normally while the organs mature, and treat the Psychonic Egg grind as a slow background project on your frigate fleet. A week of patience buys the most distinctive ship class No Man's Sky has ever shipped.
