Most No Man's Sky expeditions are not lost to difficulty — they are lost to drift. Players wander into backtracking, leave rewards unclaimed at the terminal, treat a six-week event like a six-week commitment, and then scramble when the countdown hits single digits. This No Man's Sky expedition quick completion guide exists to fix that: a repeatable, expedition-agnostic playbook for finishing any run in about two focused days, whether you are racing a fresh seasonal drop or a Redux rerun.

If you have never run an expedition at all, start with our [general expedition guide](/expeditions/expedition-guide) for how saves, phases, and reward claiming work. This page assumes you know the basics and want to go fast without making the run miserable.

## What Quick Completion Actually Means

Expeditions never ask for 100 percent. Each of the five or six phases unlocks at a milestone threshold below the total number of milestones it contains — if a phase holds twelve milestones and the threshold is eight, four of them are decoration. Quick completion is simply the discipline of hitting five or six thresholds with the minimum amount of travel, and every strategy below is a variation on that one idea.

That single rule reframes the whole event. You are not completing a checklist; you are pruning one. Before you leave your first system, you should already know which milestones you plan to ignore, which ones stack onto the same locations, and which rewards you are actually racing toward — the full pool across recent runs is catalogued in our [expedition rewards list](/expeditions/expedition-rewards-list).

## Hour One: Read, Claim, Plan

The first hour decides the length of the whole run. Speedrunners and relaxed players alike lose most of their time to the same mistake: completing objectives in the order the terminal lists them instead of the order the galaxy allows them.

1. **Read the entire milestone list at the expedition terminal before doing anything else.** Group every objective mentally by planet and system. Most wasted hours in any expedition come from backtracking to a system you passed through two hours earlier.
2. **Open the galactic map and look at the expedition route.** Every expedition parks rendezvous points along a route through the galaxy; those points anchor your fastest path. More on them below.
3. **Claim rewards immediately, every time.** Milestone and phase rewards wait at the terminal until collected, and many of them are upgrade modules and resources that make the next milestone faster. An unclaimed reward is a free upgrade you are not using.
4. **Install what you claim.** A module sitting in your cargo holds is not speeding anything up. Slot it the moment you claim it.
5. **Set a no-detour rule.** If an optional objective is more than about a minute off your route, skip it unless you are below the phase threshold. The sandbox will still be there after the event.

None of this costs extra time — steps one and two take ten minutes — and together they typically cut a run in half before you have fired your mining beam.

## The Shortest Path: Ordering Milestones by Location

Once you have read the list, order milestones by geography, not by phase. The pattern that works across essentially every expedition:

| Priority | Milestone type | Why it goes first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Objectives at your starting planet and system | Zero travel cost; clears the early phase threshold before you invest in logistics |
| 2 | Rendezvous-point milestones | The route takes you there anyway, and each rendezvous typically carries its own milestone credit |
| 3 | Travel-compatible objectives: scanning, word stones, beacons, photos | They complete themselves while you move between everything else |
| 4 | Cluster objectives: several milestones sharing one planet or system | Bundle them into a single stop instead of three trips |
| 5 | Slow single-purpose milestones: cooking chains, storm waits, rare fauna | Only do these when you are below a threshold — or bank them passively from minute one |

The stacking habit matters more than any individual route. Scan fauna while your ship refuels. Photograph waypoints as you crest hills. Interact with every knowledge stone you walk past, because word-learning milestones have appeared in multiple expeditions and they bank for free. The best runs never travel empty: every trip between two points should be retiring one or two passive milestones as a side effect.

Backtracking is the enemy to measure yourself against. A useful self-check at the top of every hour: if you are flying to a system you have already visited, ask whether an objective in the current system could have replaced that trip. If yes, your ordering slipped — recalculate before you warp.

## Rendezvous Points, the Anomaly, and Travel Shortcuts

Travel is where quick completion is won, because every milestone list in the game is trivial next to the distances between its objectives. Four mechanics do most of the work:

- **Rendezvous points.** Each expedition stages its phases along a route of rendezvous planets — dynamic hubs where the community naturally converges. Reaching them is itself worth milestone progress in most runs, and the galactic map highlights the expedition route so you can chain them. Never visit a system that is not on your way to the next rendezvous unless a cluster of milestones justifies it.
- **The Space Anomaly.** Summon it from space whenever you are between systems: it is your free claim-and-install station, your shop, and the switchboard for the trick below.
- **The Anomaly teleporter and other players' bases.** The Anomaly's teleporter is outgoing-only, but it can take you to the bases of any player currently aboard the Anomaly with you. The classic community speed trick is to hop aboard, find a player whose base sits near your next rendezvous, and teleport there instead of warping the whole way. On a busy Anomaly during launch week, this saves enormous warp chains. Space station teleporters cover the return direction — free jumps back to any system or base you have already visited, which is the cheapest way to undo a forgotten objective.
- **Portals, honestly.** The portal network almost never features in a fast expedition route, because a fresh expedition save starts with no glyphs learned. Do not chase portal unlocks mid-run; the rendezvous route plus teleporters is faster to execute than glyph hunting.

One caution from every launch week ever: new expeditions ship with bugs, and community threads during past runs have documented teleporter lists failing to show visited systems. If a shortcut misbehaves, fall back to the galactic map rather than fighting the menu — the warp route always works.

## Multiplayer: What Shares and What Doesn't

This is the most misunderstood mechanic in the format, so here is the plain version: **milestone progress is per-player.** Joining a group does not share your checklist. Every member of a fireteam must complete their own milestones and claim their own phase rewards; one veteran cannot carry four friends through the list while they watch.

| Element | Does it share? | How it actually helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milestone progress | No — per-player | Everyone climbs their own ladder, but you can climb in the same places |
| Phase rewards | No — claimed individually | Each player claims at the terminal; rewards are account-wide once claimed |
| Rides and passenger seats | Partially | Riding in a teammate's ship or exocraft skips travel and satisfies ride-style multiplayer milestones, which have appeared as optional objectives (a "Hitchhiker"-type objective in past runs) |
| Bases and infrastructure | Yes | A teammate's base near a rendezvous is a free teleport anchor and refuel point for everyone |
| Combat and resources | Yes | Group firepower trivializes combat milestones; spare resources can be traded freely |
| Community milestones | Yes — globally | These pool across every player in the event, not just your group; The Swarm's three-team war in 2026 was this idea at full scale ([Expedition 22 recap](/expeditions/expedition-22)) |

So the correct use of multiplayer is not delegation — it is logistics. A synced group moves through the same rendezvous route together, shares bases as teleport anchors, pools fuel and crafted resources, and melts combat milestones that would each cost a solo player minutes. Two players traveling together typically finish faster than either would alone, even though neither is completing the other's objectives. And if you play solo, nothing here is required: every multiplayer-flavored milestone in expedition history has been optional for phase completion.

## Upgrade Priority: Slots Before Firepower

Expedition saves start everyone on curated loadouts, which means your upgrade order matters more than your starting gear. When milestone rewards and shop stock force you to choose, spend in this order:

1. **Exosuit general slots.** Inventory is the universal bottleneck — every gather, cook, and excavation milestone moves faster with more cargo. Buy the slot available at every space station and hit drop pods you pass on-planets.
2. **Hyperdrive range.** Longer warps mean fewer of them. Install every drive-related module the run hands you before you leave a system cluster.
3. **Launch fuel economy.** Nothing burns a timetable like refueling loops on a planet you were done with. Launch-system upgrades and a habit of topping up carbon before landing fix this permanently.
4. **Hazard protection supplies, not new hazard gear.** Sodium, ion batteries, and charged protection beat shopping for suit tech in nearly every climate milestone.
5. **Weapons last.** Combat milestones are short and forgiving; only invest when a specific encounter demands it.

Resist the urge to shop for a better ship mid-run. The expedition's loadout plus claimed rewards is enough to finish every run to date — treat ship hunting as a post-expedition activity for your main save and our [best ships](/starships/best-ships) shortlist.

## The Blocker Prep List

Certain milestone types have stalled runs in every expedition since the format launched. None are hard; all are slow if you meet them unprepared. This is the pre-flight checklist for each:

| Recurring blocker | Why it stalls runs | The prep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Endure-weather objectives | Storms and heat spikes run on their own schedule, not yours | Arrive with hazard protection fully charged; do nearby scanning or gathering objectives while you wait instead of staring at the sky |
| Storm-only resources | Some gatherable materials only appear during storms | Fold them into the same weather wait — never schedule a separate trip |
| Cooking and processing chains | Multi-step recipes need precursors you won't have on you | Read recipe requirements off the milestone list at the start; gather precursors passively while traveling |
| Word-learning milestones | Requires interacting with knowledge stones over time | Touch every knowledge stone and monolith you pass from minute one — this banks progress for free |
| Rare fauna scanning | Some discovery milestones need specific creature types that are uncommon | Scan everything that moves early; partial discovery progress carries into the late phases |
| Set-piece combat encounters | Boss-style milestones punish arriving under-prepared | Arrive with charged weapons, shields, and launch fuel; a death costs a full trip back |

The common thread: blockers punish players who meet them cold. Ten minutes with the milestone list at the terminal tells you which of these your run contains and where they sit, and every one of them can be pre-loaded onto travel you were doing anyway.

## The Two-Day Timeline

Here is what a comfortable two-day clear looks like, hour-budgeted. It assumes normal pace, no prior experience with the specific run, and a day-one community route open in a browser tab — optimized routes appear on Reddit and YouTube within days of every launch, and even a spoiler-light skeleton saves hours.

| Block | Budget | What you finish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1, session 1 | 2–3 hours | Start the save, read the full milestone list, plan the route, clear the entire first phase plus most of the second around the starting system and first rendezvous |
| Day 1, session 2 | 2 hours | Chain the next two rendezvous points, claim and install everything, bank passive milestones (scans, stones, photos) while crossing planets |
| Day 2, session 1 | 2–3 hours | Reach the final phase's rendezvous, clear remaining thresholds, backfill any blocker milestones you have been banking |
| Day 2, session 2 | 1 hour | Finale milestone, full claim sweep at the terminal — verify every phase reads claimed — then load your main save and collect unlocked items from the Quicksilver Synthesis Companion |

Total: seven to nine hours across two days, which comfortably absorbs one bad storm wait, one bug, and one session that runs short. Compress the blocks into a single day and you have the standard two-to-four-hour optimized clear; stretch it and you are exactly where the six-to-ten-hour relaxed run lives.

## What the Speedrun Records Teach

No Man's Sky expedition speedrunning is a real, documented discipline — speedrun.com maintains leaderboards for the format, including full-run and individual-phase categories. The Expedition 1: The Pioneers full-run record stands at 1 hour 29 minutes 38 seconds, phase records run as low as 34 minutes, and video guides exist for clearing specific expeditions in around 90 minutes. Community routes regularly land recent runs in the same territory: the Remnant expedition was widely cleared in about three hours with a published route ([Remnant route breakdown](/expeditions/expedition-21-remnant-guide)).

The instructive part is what those runs do not rely on: reflexes, combat skill, or exotic gear. They are planning artifacts. Route knowledge, aggressive claiming, and ruthless skipping account for nearly the entire gap between a ninety-minute clear and a ten-hour one — which is why the same methods scale down perfectly to a two-day casual clear. You are not practicing mechanics; you are avoiding waste.
