For ten years, the answer to "can you land on stars in No Man's Sky" was a flat no. Stars were painted light on the skybox, not physical places, and no amount of pulse engine flying would ever take you to one. The Cosmos update, announced on August 9, 2026 as the game's tenth anniversary centerpiece, changes that: Hello Games has confirmed you can land on a star, supported by newly announced heat-resistant technology.

**Last updated: August 16, 2026.** Full patch notes are still being released in stages, so this guide separates what is confirmed, what is reasonable speculation clearly labeled as such, and what is simply unknown. We will revise as the remaining details are published.

## Could You Ever Land on Stars Before Cosmos?

No, and the reasons are worth understanding because they explain why this feature is such a big deal. Historically, a star system in No Man's Sky was a bounded space. You could fly freely within it, land on every planet and moon, and pulse between them, but the star itself was a visual element rendered at an unreachable distance. Community threads consistently confirmed that stars were not interactive objects.

Interstellar travel reinforced the boundary. The only passage between systems was the hyperdrive: charge warp cells, plot a route on the galaxy map, and jump through what was effectively a loading screen. The void between stars, and the stars themselves, simply were not part of playable space. Cosmos breaks both rules at once, which is why star landings and interstellar free flight were announced together.

## What Cosmos Has Confirmed About Star Landings

The confirmed facts are narrow but solid. Hello Games announced, as part of the tenth anniversary reveal and Sean Murray's open letter to the community, that the Cosmos update lets players land on a star, and that new heat-resistant equipment or technology is part of the package. Those two statements are the foundation of everything else.

What has not been published yet is the gameplay layer: how you initiate a landing, what a stellar surface actually contains, whether resources, hazards, anomalies, or bases exist there, whether different star classes such as red or blue systems behave differently, and what failure looks like if your protection runs out. ## Heat-Resistant Gear: What Hello Games Has Said

The announcement ties star landings to heat-resistant technology, which is the only supporting system confirmed by name. No recipes, module names, slot requirements, or unlock conditions have been detailed yet, and the remaining patch notes are expected to fill that gap.

What we can say confidently is that No Man's Sky already has a mature framework for environmental survival, and Hello Games has a decade-long habit of extending existing frameworks rather than replacing them. Extreme heat, cold, radiation, and toxicity on planets are handled today through the Exosuit's hazard protection system: a baseline shield, specialized protection modules per hazard type, tiered upgrade modules from technology merchants, and in-field recharging that consumes resources. Any stellar protection layer will be judged against that standard, because veteran players will expect it to interact with the systems they already maintain.

## How Star Protection Could Fit Your Exosuit: Speculation

This section is explicitly speculation. It draws only on long-established game systems to sketch plausible implementations, and it should be read as preparation guidance, not fact.

The most likely model, based on how prior hazard systems were introduced, is a new protection tier sitting above the existing heat module line. When extreme planets arrived, they required dedicated modules beyond baseline shielding; a star's surface is the ultimate version of that curve. A stellar protection module could occupy Exosuit technology slots, consume recharging resources during a landing much as hazard protection drains in extreme weather, and possibly gate landings behind a technology quest or crafting chain.

| Existing Exosuit system | What it does today | How star tech could extend it |
|------------------------|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Base hazard protection | Absorbs general environmental damage | The underlying shield a stellar tier would stack on |
| Heat protection modules | Defend against extreme heat and cold | The direct ancestor of star-heat shielding |
| Protection upgrade modules | Raise strength and duration of shields | Tiered stellar upgrades following the same pattern |
| Technology slot expansion | More room for installed modules | Extra slots to fit new star gear alongside old kit |
| Field recharging resources | Restore protection mid-expedition | Likely consumed continuously during a landing |

The practical takeaway is the same under any implementation: a save with deep Exosuit protection investment, generous technology slots, and strong recharging logistics will be better positioned for star landings than a fresh one, whatever the exact module names turn out to be.

## Preparing for Your First Star Landing: Checklist

You can complete most of this preparation today using systems that have been stable for years. Nothing here is wasted effort even if the details shift when full patch notes arrive.

| Preparation | Why it matters | Priority |
|-------------|----------------|----------|
| Maximize Exosuit hazard protection capacity | The deeper your protection pool, the longer you survive any heat drain | High |
| Install or upgrade heat protection modules | The closest existing analog to stellar heat | High |
| Stockpile recharging resources for protection and life support | Field recharge loops are how every hazard expedition survives | High |
| Bank units and nanites | New modules typically cost currency or blueprints | High |
| Upgrade your starship's shields and launch systems | The approach and departure matter as much as the landing | Medium |
| Unlock technology merchants on the Space Anomaly | Upgrade modules historically come from merchant stock | Medium |
| Keep a freighter stocked with backup gear | A mobile resupply point near the star system you target | Low |

Two items deserve emphasis. First, resource logistics: every extreme environment in the game rewards carrying recharge materials rather than relying on returns to shelter, and a stellar surface will be the least forgiving environment yet attempted. Second, do not over-invest in any single guess about module names. Broaden the base of your protection build rather than gambling on one predicted recipe.

Yes, as an announced Cosmos update feature. Hello Games confirmed star landings as part of the tenth anniversary announcement on August 9, 2026. ### Will my ship burn up on the approach?

Unknown. The announcement pairs star landings with heat-resistant technology without specifying whether that gear is Exosuit-based, ship-based, or both. Ship shields and launch systems are worth upgrading as insurance, but no requirement has been confirmed.

### Is landing on stars dangerous for newer players?

Probably, once details arrive, since stellar heat will presumably exceed anything planets produce. Nothing published so far indicates a hard progression gate, though. A mid-game save with solid hazard protection appears well positioned, and we will update this answer as requirements are confirmed.

### Can you build a base on a star?

No official statement covers this yet. Base building content is reported as part of Cosmos's anniversary additions, and stars becoming physical spaces invites the question naturally, but any answer today would be speculation. Watch the full patch notes and base building menus once the update is fully live.
