Ask a No Man's Sky veteran what the multitool is and they will describe a mining laser. Ask them what it becomes after thirty hours and the answer changes shape entirely: eight distinct weapon modes, from an automatic rifle to a charge-cannon to a grenade launcher, all bolted to the same chassis that cuts rocks. The weapon system is one of the game's quietest depth pockets — and one of its easiest to build wrong, because the [three-module family limit](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/) means specialization isn't optional.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. This guide explains every weapon mode — how it fires, what it costs, what it's for — and ranks them for a 2026 combat build.

## The Eight Weapon Modes at a Glance

| Mode | Fire type | Ammunition | Role |
|------|-----------|------------|------|
| Mining Beam | Continuous beam | None | Mining first, weapon second |
| Boltcaster | Automatic rifle | Crafted ammo | The all-purpose primary |
| Scatter Blaster | Shotgun burst | Crafted ammo | Point-blank devastation |
| Pulse Spitter | Arcing projectiles | Projectile ammo | Mid-range, weak-point shots |
| Blaze Javelin | Charged beam | Charge only | Precision sniper |
| Plasma Launcher | Lobbed grenade | Grenades | AoE structure clearing |
| Geology Cannon | Explosive projectile | Projectile ammo | Terrain destruction, close bursts |
| Neutron Cannon | Charge-shot orb | Charge only | The no-ammo powerhouse |

## Boltcaster: The Default Answer

The boltcaster is the mode the game assumes you fight with: fully automatic, honest range, cheap ammunition crafted from common materials, and the deepest upgrade shelf in the game. Its ceiling with three S-class damage modules handles pirates, Sentinel waves, and the corrupted drones of dissonant planets without exotic technique. If you only ever build one weapon line, this is it.

## Neutron Cannon: The Connoisseur's Choice

The neutron cannon trades ammunition logistics for charge discipline: hold to charge, release to launch an orb that scales with charge time. Fully upgraded it hits harder than the boltcaster, needs no ammo crafting mid-expedition, and its splash forgivingly clips enemies you aimed slightly past. The cost is pace — it rewards deliberate positioning over run-and-gun. Many endgame combat builds, especially on a [sentinel multitool](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/), run it as primary.

## Scatter Blaster: The Room-Clearer

A shotgun: brutal inside kissing distance, useless beyond it. Its niche is real — corrupted drone swarms that rush you, Sentinel quads that close to melee range, fauna that didn't appreciate being scanned — and a scatter-blast to the face solves all three. As a *second* weapon family with modest module investment it's a delight; as a primary it asks you to fight everything at handshake range.

## Pulse Spitter: The Mid-Range Specialist

Three-round bursts on a flat arc — the pulse spitter is the tool for enemies at the range where the scatter blaster dies and the boltcaster spreads. It rewards trigger discipline and weak-point aim, and its ammunition is a single craftable type. Solid, unspectacular, and the first mode most builds sacrifice when module slots run short.

## Blaze Javelin: The Sniper

A charge-up precision beam with range the other modes envy. The javelin one-shots soft targets at distance and rewards patience, but the charge time punishes you in close fights, and the game's combat rarely demands the range it offers. Players who love it build their whole tool around it; everyone else installs it and forgets it.

## Plasma Launcher and Geology Cannon: The Explosives

Both launch AoE ordnance. The plasma launcher lobs grenades — the structure-and-crowd tool, at home clearing Sentinel deployables and clustered pirates. The geology cannon doubles as a terrain tool: it digs, demolishes, and un-buries, which gives it a permanent utility slot in many toolbars even when its damage falls behind the rifles. As primaries both are meme-adjacent; as utilities both earn their keep.

## Mining Beam: The Weapon You Already Have

Often forgotten in weapon talk: the mining beam damages enemies with zero ammunition. It is the weakest damage mode, but it never runs dry — the classic fallback when a wave outlasts your ammo, and genuinely deadly on a tool built around [mining beam upgrades](/multitools/mining-beam-upgrades/). Its real job is mining, and no weapon discussion changes that.

## Building a Combat Tool in 2026

The module limit makes the decision structural:

1. **Pick one primary family** — boltcaster or neutron cannon — and give it three S-class damage modules, clustered for adjacency, strongest in a supercharged slot.
2. **Pick one supplement** — scatter blaster for close emergencies, geology cannon for utility — with light or no module investment.
3. **Keep the mining beam line on the same tool** — a combat tool that can't mine is a tool you'll swap constantly.
4. **Match the tool to the job:** a good alien roll or the [sentinel multitool](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/) for the combat identity, an [experimental](/multitools/experimental-multitool/) if this doubles as your scanner. The full tool-type ranking is in [best multitools](/multitools/best-multitools/).

The 2026 combat tempo — sharper dogfighting since the May Swarm update, denser corrupted-planet waves in the dissonance loop — has rewarded the neutron cannon's no-ammo rhythm more than in earlier years, but the honest answer remains: the best weapon mode is the one with your three best modules in it.
