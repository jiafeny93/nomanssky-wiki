Ask a hundred No Man's Sky players their favorite base planet and you will hear about sunsets, bioluminescent grass, floating islands. Ask them about their *second* base and you will hear strategy: the frozen world with the power hotspot, the dissonant planet next to the interceptor crash, the scorched hell that grows the plants worth millions. Picking a base planet is a real decision in this game — the Base Computer is a commitment, deletion refunds nothing, and the right site does half your base's job for you. Here is how to choose like a veteran.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For raising the base once you have the site, see the [base building guide](/base-building/base-building-guide/); for the claim-and-restore mechanics, [Base Computer placement](/base-building/base-computer-placement/).

## The Five Criteria

Check them in order — the list runs from "fine to skip" to "decides everything":

1. **Weather.** Storms kill visibility, interrupt outdoor work, and drain hazard protection. A world with no or rare storms is calm forever; a "temperate" label with constant superstorms is a daily tax.
2. **Sentinels.** Passive-sentinel worlds let you mine and build in peace. High-security worlds make every errand an escalating firefight. Bases are never raided — this is about your *attention*, not your property.
3. **Resource mix.** The elements you actually consume — carbon, ferrite, the metals your [refining loops](/guides/refining-recipes/) want — within walking distance.
4. **Hotspots.** The big one: electromagnetic power hotspots and mineral hotspots, findable on foot with the Survey Device. A strong power hotspot means the base never thinks about energy again; a gold or indium-class mineral hotspot means the base *pays you* to live there. Survey before you commit — see [electrical wiring](/base-building/electrical-wiring-guide/).
5. **Sightlines and terrain.** Flat ground, a view, interesting geology to build against. The tiebreaker — satisfying, but downstream of the four above.

## Reading a Planet Before Landing

The work starts in orbit. The galaxy map and the planet's discoverable profile tell you weather adjectives, sentinel level, and resource tags before you commit a landing. In practice:

| Signal | Read it as |
|--------|-----------|
| "Paradise," "verdant," gentle weather words | Home-base material |
| "Extreme," "frenzied storms," fire/ice superlatives | Functional-world material — or skip |
| "Aggressive sentinels" | Every outdoor chore becomes combat |
| Activated metal tag | Extreme world, premium ore — factory site |
| Dissonant system | Corrupted planets — [sentinel loot](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/) country |

Then land and verify: scan the environment, watch a full weather cycle if you can, and run a Survey Device sweep of the actual build site. Orbit tells you the climate; only boots on the ground tell you the neighbors — hotspots, terrain, and whether the grass is really that color.

## Paradise Versus Everything Else

The game's quiet joke is that the prettiest worlds are often the least useful. Paradise planets have gentle weather and calm sentinels and that is all they have — the valuable stuff concentrates at the extremes: activated metals on storm worlds, [dissonance loot](/multitools/best-multitools/) on corrupted planets, rare flora in exotic biomes.

The mature answer is a portfolio:

- **The home base** — paradise criteria: calm, pretty, with the [storage hub](/base-building/storage-containers/) and the teleporter nexus.
- **The power base** — wherever the S-class electromagnetic hotspot lives; climate irrelevant, the base is a power plant with a room attached.
- **The farm** — a world that grows what your craft chain needs.
- **The mining outpost** — a mineral hotspot or activated-metal world; teleport in, fill the containers, teleport out.
- **The dissonance camp** — a corrupted-planet forward base for the Echo Locator loop.

Each is small. Together they outproduce any single megabase — and the per-base object limit means the portfolio is also the only way to build at scale.

## Aesthetic Picks Worth the Trouble

Some sites are simply worth owning. A floating island over a green sea. A lakeshore with the sunset behind the mountains. A cave mouth that becomes a hangar. The trick with showcase sites is the same as with factories — **survey first**: the dream island with no power hotspot and no metal nearby will be beautiful and useless, and you will love it anyway, but you should love it knowingly. Long power runs and periodic [resource hauls](/base-building/base-building-tips/) are the price of a view; pay it on purpose.

## The Checklist, Compressed

Before placing the Base Computer, on the actual site:

- Weather observed calm, or the base is designed to ignore it
- Sentinels passive, or you enjoy the fight
- EM hotspot surveyed and inside the build radius, or accepted its absence
- Mineral hotspot checked — a paying site beats a free one
- Terrain flattens without terraforming a mountain
- The view. Last, on purpose.

Your home planet is the one place in a galaxy of eighteen quintillion planets that is yours. Pick it the way you would pick a house — function, neighbors, power — and then, having been sensible, absolutely let the sunset break the tie.
