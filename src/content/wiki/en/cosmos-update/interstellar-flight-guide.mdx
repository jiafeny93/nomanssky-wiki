Interstellar flight in No Man's Sky has meant exactly one thing for a decade: warping. The Cosmos update, announced on August 9, 2026 for the game's tenth anniversary, introduces the option to fly straight to a different solar system under your own power. It is one of the most fundamental changes the game has ever received, and this guide covers how it fits alongside the warp systems you already know.

**Last updated: August 16, 2026.** Hello Games is publishing Cosmos patch notes in stages, so confirmed facts, stated expectations, and open questions are labeled separately throughout. We will update as the remaining mechanics are detailed.

## Interstellar Flight Before Cosmos: Warp or Nothing

To appreciate what changes, start with the old rules. Inside a star system, your starship has always been free: pulse between planets, dogfight pirates, mine asteroids, and land anywhere. But the boundary of the system was hard. The star was a skybox element, distant systems were unreachable points of light, and the only passage out was the hyperdrive.

That warp workflow has been stable for ten years. You craft or buy warp cells to fuel the hyperdrive, open the galaxy map, select a system within your drive's jump range, and execute the jump. Range scales with hyperdrive upgrade modules, freighters jump farther and carry your fleet along, and certain star classes have long required specialized drive technology to reach. It is a fast, menu-driven system, and its one limitation is that the journey itself does not exist. Space between stars was never a place you could be.

## What Cosmos Changes for Interstellar Travel

The announcement from Hello Games is direct: the Cosmos update gives you the option to fly straight to a different solar system. Free interstellar flight turns the void between stars into traversable space for the first time, converting a galaxy of instanced bubbles into one continuous volume.

Three aspects of the old model are directly affected. First, distance becomes real in a way it never was, which raises design questions around scaling that the staged patch notes have not fully answered. Second, the galaxy map's role may shift from a teleport selector toward a genuine route planner, since knowing what lies between you and a destination finally matters. Third, arrival stops being instant, so what you can do mid-flight, scan, manage your ship, or simply watch the stars move, becomes a gameplay question. The intent is confirmed. The specifics of speed, fuel draw, and mid-flight hazards are arriving piece by piece.

## Fuel and Hyperdrive Costs: What to Expect

No fuel model for free interstellar flight has been published yet, so treat this section as preparation built on the game's economic patterns rather than confirmed mechanics.

The existing travel economy gives strong hints. In-system flight uses the pulse engine fed by launch fuel and pulse spares; interstellar jumps use warp cells crafted from antimatter and antimatter housing; and upgrades on both systems reduce cost and extend range. A third consumption model for sustained interstellar cruising would fit the pattern of drawing on resources you can gather, craft, or buy, which argues for a well-stocked ship before a long free flight. Expect the classic efficiency curve to apply as well: better drive technology should reduce whatever per-distance cost free flight charges, just as hyperdrive modules stretch jump range.

The practical preparation is unglamorous and effective. Keep warp cells in stock, because free flight is framed as an option and the hyperdrive is expected to keep its role. Carry surplus fuel and crafting materials, and upgrade your drive modules now; investment in range and efficiency has paid off across every travel system the game has shipped.

## Navigation Tips for Long Interstellar Flights

Navigation advice for free flight is necessarily provisional until mechanics are confirmed, but the following principles are low risk because they rest on systems that already exist.

| Tip | Why it works |
|-----|--------------|
| Plot the same route on the galaxy map first | The map's economy and conflict data remains the best source on a destination |
| Prefer runs that pass through familiar systems | Known space gives you resupply points and rescue options |
| Top off fuel, shields, and life support before departing | Every long-range system in the game punishes leaving half prepared |
| Upgrade hyperdrive modules even for free flight | Drive quality has always governed interstellar capability |
| Confirm freighter support at the destination | A freighter remains the logistics hub for extended expeditions |
| Check economic and conflict data on target systems | Reported conditions have guided routing since the map added them |

Two habits deserve special emphasis. First, always have a warp fallback: until free flight mechanics are fully known, the hyperdrive is your guarantee against being stranded far from help. Second, treat your first long interstellar flight as a shakedown rather than a commitment. Test fuel consumption and travel time on a short hop to a neighboring system before attempting anything ambitious.

## Who Should Fly Free and Who Should Keep Warping

Free interstellar flight is an option, not an obligation, and different players will get different value from it.

Explorers are the obvious audience. If your favorite moments in No Man's Sky are the quiet ones, the unbroken cruise through space, the sense of a continuous galaxy, free flight was designed for you. Photographers and travelers chasing the scale of the void gain an entirely new medium. Players who enjoy slow, deliberate expeditions with self-imposed logistics will find a new genre of trip to plan.

Efficiency-minded players lose nothing. Warp jumps are expected to remain the fastest way between systems, traders running circuit routes and farmers hopping between bases should notice no mandatory change, and commuters who play in short sessions may rarely touch free flight at all. The update widens the toolbox rather than replacing it. If you are returning for the anniversary, the honest recommendation is to try one deliberate free flight to a neighboring system, then let your own preferences decide how often you use the option.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you fly between star systems in No Man's Sky without warping?

As an announced feature of the Cosmos update, yes. Hello Games confirmed that the option to fly straight to a different solar system is part of the tenth anniversary update announced on August 9, 2026. Before Cosmos this was categorically impossible; the exact mechanics of free flight are still being published in staged patch notes.

### Does interstellar free flight use warp cells?

That has not been confirmed either way. Warp cells remain the hyperdrive's fuel, and since free flight is framed as an option rather than a replacement, warping is expected to keep its cost model. What, if anything, free flight consumes is one of the details still pending in the patch notes.

### Is free flight faster than warping?

Almost certainly not, based on everything announced. Warp is an instant jump while free flight involves actually crossing the distance, so expect warping to remain the efficiency choice. Treat free flight as the scenic and exploratory option until confirmed otherwise.

### Can I still use the galaxy map to plan routes?

The galaxy map is expected to remain central to navigation, since it is the only source of system data on economy, conflict, and star class. How it integrates with free flight routing, whether it plots intermediate waypoints, for example, is not yet detailed.

### Do I need a freighter for interstellar free flight?

Nothing announced suggests a freighter is required. Freighters have historically enhanced long-range travel by carrying ships, resources, and mobile bases along, and they remain excellent support for extended expeditions, but no free flight requirement involving them has been published. Expect a well-equipped starship to be sufficient, with details to be confirmed.
