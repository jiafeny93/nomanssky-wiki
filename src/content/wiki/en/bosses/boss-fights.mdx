No Man's Sky never shows you a boss health bar. Its hardest fights are hidden inside escalation systems — push a Sentinel planet too far, defend a freighter too long, or board the wrong derelict — and a boss-level encounter walks out of a system you thought you understood. There are four of them, and each one has a counter.

## What actually counts as a boss in No Man's Sky?

Four encounters behave like bosses: a fixed escalation instead of random danger, a dramatic entrance, a clear win condition, and loot worth the trouble.

1. **Sentinel Walker** — the ground boss, deployed at maximum wanted level
2. **Sentinel Dreadnought** — the space capital ship, arriving on the fifth wave
3. **Pirate capital ships** — dreadnoughts leading freighter raids in wealthy systems
4. **Derelict freighter horrors** — the PvE gauntlet in the dark

## Sentinel Walker: the ground boss

On extreme-Sentinel planets, keep fighting past the drones and quadrupeds and hardframes. At maximum wanted level, the two-legged Walker deploys — a walking fortress that community consensus names the hardest ground enemy in the game.

The fight has one rule: **the leg armor goes first**. Shots into the body plating mostly bounce; strip a leg's armor and the exposed internals underneath take real damage. Paralysis munitions (stun grenades) lock the Walker in place for several seconds each — two or three buys you a full damage window on the broken leg.

<Callout type="tip" title="Bring the right gun">
An upgraded boltcaster or neutron cannon shortens this fight dramatically — veterans kill Walkers in seconds once the weapon tree is filled in. A plasma launcher helps during the hardframe waves that precede it. If your weapons are still stock, this is the wrong boss to duel: break off, lose the heat, and come back armed.
</Callout>

<Accordion title="Surviving the escalation to max wanted">
The Walker is the fifth act of a longer fight. Quadrupeds hit hard at range; hardframes close distance fast and hit harder. Fight near cover you can put between you and the lasers, keep hazard protection topped up, and use the pause between waves to reload and recharge — the Walker deploys only after hardframes fall, so arriving at it with empty shields is the classic mistake.
</Accordion>

## Sentinel Dreadnought: the capital-ship boss

In space, attack a Sentinel presence — a planetary resonator, or Sentinel freighter subsystems — and hold your ground. Defeat four waves of Sentinel interceptors and the **Dreadnought warps in as the fifth wave**, and the community is not joking when it files this ship next to the Walker as the game's toughest encounter.

Capital ships are won from inside the trench:

1. **Fly into the lengthwise hull trench** — the deep channel running along the Dreadnought's spine, where most of its guns cannot track you.
2. **Destroy the ball-shaped shield generators** lining the trench. Shields stay up until the last generator pops.
3. **Kill the core** with your starship weapons once the shields drop.

The reward loop is why people farm it: each kill drops a **Dreadnought AI Fragment** (a crafting component), and winning gives you a shot at boarding and **claiming the Dreadnought as your own capital freighter** — with random class rolls sending S-class hunters back for repeat fights.

## Pirate capital ships: the freighter-rescue boss

When pirates raid a civilian freighter in a wealthy system, a pirate dreadnought can lead the assault — and it dies by the same doctrine as its Sentinel counterpart: into the trench, shield generators first, then the core. The warp-in of a capital ship mid-rescue is one of the game's genuine "boss entrance" moments, and saving the freighter pays standing with the race whose ship you protected.

<Callout type="warn" title="Escalation is wealth-dependent">
Capital ships in pirate raids show up in richer systems. If your rescues are all fighter waves, you are fighting in systems too poor to field dreadnoughts — jump up the economy ladder.
</Callout>

## Derelict freighters: the horror gauntlet

Derelict freighters are NMS's dungeon run — and its closest thing to a horror game. Power is out, gravity is on, and the dark is occupied: minifiends swarm from nests, green jellyfish drift through zero-g corridors, rogue AIs blink between rooms, abyssal horrors fill flooded decks, and ceiling turrets chew through anyone who sprints.

Nothing here has a health bar, but the encounter plays like a boss rush. The counters are unglamorous and effective: bring ammo (lots of it), top off hazard protection before boarding, keep a geology cannon or similar area weapon for nest clusters, and clear rooms slowly — the player who sprints is the player who dies. Our [freighter guide](/starships/freighter-guide/) covers how derelict runs fit into building your fleet.

## Which fight should you take on first?

If your weapons are upgraded: the pirate dreadnought — it is the most forgiving trench run and pays fleet progress. If your weapons are excellent: farm the Sentinel Dreadnought for the freighter claim. The Walker waits until you enjoy pain, and derelicts wait until you have ammo to burn. None of them are mandatory — but they are the closest thing this game has to final exams.
