Ask a No Man's Sky player what they farm and you learn their whole save. Hour ten: scanning money. Hour fifty: chlorine in a refiner. Hour two hundred: a grid of extractors on a hell-planet nobody visits, quietly minting more units than they can spend. Unit farming in this game has a tech tree of its own — each loop cheaper per unit than the last, until the problem inverts and the question becomes what to buy. This guide ranks the loops that matter in 2026, from the first scan to the farm that outlives your interest.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For the strategy overview, see [money making](/guides/money-making-guide/); this page is the farming-loops companion — the builds, the upkeep, and what each one pays.

## The Loop Ranking at a Glance

| Loop | Setup | Upkeep | Payout character |
|------|-------|--------|------------------|
| Scanner modules | Buy 3 modules | None | Instant, active, early-king |
| Chlorine expansion | Refiners + oxygen | Feed refiners | Active printing, cheap setup |
| Craft chains | Refiners + blueprints | Material logistics | High ceiling, semi-passive |
| Activated indium farm | Hotspot + base build | Almost none | Passive millions |
| Expedition rewards | None | Time-limited | Burst income + assets |

## 1. The Scanner Loop: First Money, Still Good Money

The humblest loop and the best early one: install three S-class Scanner Upgrade modules on any multitool, and every creature, plant, and mineral scan pays sums that make the early game trivial. Completing a planet's full fauna set stacks a bonus on top.

Why it tops the early ranking: zero upkeep, works everywhere, and the [module discipline](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/) costs a station or two of shopping. A fresh traveler with a upgraded scanner can fund their first real purchases from one planet's wildlife. It never scales to megabase money — but it buys the tools that do.

## 2. Chlorine Expansion: The Active Printer

The classic multiplication loop: salt and oxygen refine into chlorine, and chlorine plus more oxygen refines into *more* chlorine. Inputs are cheap and abundant — oxygen harvests in fat red plants everywhere and refines from kelp sacs and cave fodder — and the loop needs only a bank of refiners on base power.

Its character is active income: the refiners do the multiplying while you feed them, and a stocked [refining base](/guides/refining-recipes/) turns an hour of oxygen gathering into a stack of chlorine worth selling in any three-star system. Best ratio of setup cost to return in the game for players who prefer playing to building.

## 3. Craft Chains: The Ceiling Raiser

Raw materials sell as raw materials forever; walked down a recipe chain they multiply at every step. The veteran shape: refiners converting ore and gas into components, crafters turning components into high-value trade goods, storage buffering the pipeline. The famous endgame chains — the stasis-device-and-living-glass family — require scattered blueprints and rare materials, and pay accordingly.

The character is semi-passive and fiddly: the [refining guide](/guides/refining-recipes/) covers the machine side, but the chain's throughput lives on logistics — gas collectors, frost crystal cultivation, and a [storage hub](/base-building/storage-containers/) that keeps stages separated. Highest skill ceiling in the units game; the loops below out-earn it for less thought.

## 4. The Activated Indium Farm: Passive King

The endgame answer and the loop that retires the question. On an extreme-weather planet with an **activated indium hotspot**, a base of electromagnetic generators, mineral extractors, and supply depots harvests the deposit into storage continuously. You teleport in on any cadence, collect, and teleport out; the deposit never depletes.

The build is the whole cost: a survey for the [hotspot pair](/base-building/best-base-planets/), the power and extractor grid ([electrical guide](/base-building/electrical-wiring-guide/)), and the patience to wire it. After that it is a money faucet with no moving parts — millions per collection cycle at full scale, running while you are offline. Every other loop on this page is what you did *before* you built one.

## 5. Expeditions and Events: The Burst

Time-limited expeditions hand out ships, multitools, cosmetics, and stacks of tradeable rewards at rates the permanent game never offers — a single [expedition run](/expeditions/expedition-guide/) can drop assets worth millions plus pure cash rewards. Nexus missions and weekend events fill the gaps between expeditions with steady unit payouts and the community-currency side income ([Quicksilver](/guides/quicksilver-farming/)).

Not a farm in the steady sense — a harvest calendar. But a returning player who rides expeditions hard banks more value in two weeks than a pure farm produces in a month.

## Choosing by Save Stage

- **First 10 hours:** scanner loop, plus the [starter tips](/guides/starter-tips/) economy habits. Buy suit slots.
- **Hours 10–50:** chlorine expansion and first craft chains; begin the base ([building guide](/base-building/base-building-guide/)) that later loops assume.
- **Hours 50+:** survey for the indium farm, build it, and let it fund [S-class ship hunting](/starships/s-class-ships/), freighter fleets, and everything after.

The quiet truth: units stop mattering. A working farm makes every price in the game a rounding error, and the game's real currencies become time and attention — which is the point at which you are free to play whatever you actually enjoy. The farm is not the goal; the farm is what buys the goal.
