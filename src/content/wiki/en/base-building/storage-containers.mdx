Somewhere around hour thirty, every No Man's Sky player hits the same wall: a full inventory, a full ship, and a bank of exosuit upgrades that still is not enough. The answer is not more suit slots — it is the storage system. Ten numbered containers, buildable at every base, sharing inventories across the galaxy. It is one of the game's quietest quality-of-life systems and one of the easiest to set up badly, because the organization scheme you choose at Container 0 is the one you will live with for the rest of the save.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For the base those containers live in, start with the [base building guide](/base-building/base-building-guide/).

## The Ten Containers

Storage in No Man's Sky means **Storage Containers 0 through 9**, unlocked through construction research (salvaged data) in the build menu — see [base parts unlock](/base-building/base-parts-unlock/) for the research economy. Each container holds **twenty slots**, a full set is two hundred, and they are buildable at every base you own plus as freighter rooms on your [capital ship](/starships/freighter-guide/).

The part that changes how you play is not the capacity. It is the sharing.

## Same Number, Same Inventory

Every container with the same number opens the **same inventory, everywhere**:

- Container 3 at your farm base, Container 3 at your mountain retreat, Container 3's freighter room — three doors into one box.
- Drop loot into any copy from anywhere in the galaxy; pick it up from any other copy later.
- Nothing teleports, nothing syncs with a delay — they are simply the same storage, addressed by number.

This turns your teleporter network into a logistics system. The loop every veteran runs: fill up on [expedition or farming loot](/guides/money-making-guide/), teleport to the base with the storage hub, dump into the numbered containers, teleport back to the action with a clean inventory. Done.

## Building the Storage Hub

The classic implementation, in build order:

1. **One enclosed room** — cuboid or prefab — at your main base, next to the [teleporter](/base-building/base-building-guide/).
2. **All ten containers** inside, in numerical order, powered and connected.
3. **Labels.** Decals, signs, or the containers' own numbered decals — anything that says what lives where from the doorway.
4. **A copy of your highest-traffic numbers** at secondary bases. If Container 6 is "farm output," a Container 6 at the farm saves the main-base round trip.

The hub costs an evening. It repays that evening every single play session for the rest of the game.

## An Organization Scheme That Survives

The categories that hold up at two hundred hours, one per number:

| Container | Category | Why it holds up |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| 0 | Metals and ores | The bulk of every mining loop |
| 1 | Plants and farm output | Farming runs arrive in floods |
| 2 | Activated and exotic metals | Valuable enough to deserve their own number |
| 3 | Technology and modules | Upgrade-module shopping ends in stockpiles |
| 4 | Fuel — cells, warp materials | Restock runs start here |
| 5 | Crafted goods and trade items | The [units](/guides/money-making-guide/) pipeline |
| 6 | Building materials | Ferrite, carbon, chromatic metal — the builder's pantry |
| 7 | Consumables and hazard protection | What you restock before expeditions |
| 8 | Sentinels and dissonance loot | The corrupted-planet haul |
| 9 | Curiosities and "later" | The honest junk drawer |

Tune the categories to your save, but keep the discipline: **one number, one meaning, everywhere.** The scheme only works because a number is the same box galaxy-wide; a container that means "metals here, modules there" is a trap you set for your future self.

## Storage Beyond the Containers

The containers sit inside a wider storage stack worth knowing:

- **Exosuit** — your on-body slots, expandable with drop pod coordinates and upgrades; the thing you are freeing up.
- **Ship inventory** — the biggest early-game overflow, and cargo slots hold more per stack.
- **Freighter rooms** — container rooms aboard the freighter mirror your numbered containers; the freighter becomes the storage hub you bring with you.
- **Refiners as buffer** — not storage, but every veteran has parked a stack in a refiner during a sorting session. (The [refining guide](/guides/refining-recipes/) covers what refiners are actually for.)

## Mistakes to Skip

- **Unlabeled containers.** Ten identical boxes with no signs is a riddle you re-solve every session.
- **Categories by destination instead of kind.** "Stuff for the farm base" decays the moment plans change; "plants" never does.
- **One mega-mixed container.** The junk-drawer instinct is real — contain it in one number (9) rather than spreading it across ten.
- **Building storage far from the teleporter.** Every step between arrival point and container is a step you take thousands of times.
- **Forgetting the freighter copy.** The same-numbered freighter room is the whole system's mobile half; one [freighter](/starships/freighter-guide/) room turns your capital ship into the arrival point.

Two hundred slots, one room, one scheme. Set it up this weekend and your inventory stops being a problem for the rest of the game — which leaves more time for the parts of No Man's Sky that are actually [worth playing](/guides/worth-playing-2026/).
