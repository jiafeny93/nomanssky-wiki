Every hobby in No Man's Sky eventually becomes a collection hobby, and no multitool is collected harder than the royal. Gold filigree over a black chassis, a spawn rate low enough that most players see their first one after dozens of hours, and stats good enough to actually carry — the royal is the multitool pool's exotic ship. It is also the tool most misunderstood by new hunters, who assume rare means best. It does not, quite. Here is what the royal actually is, where it comes from, and whether it deserves your reloads.

**Last updated: August 22, 2026**, current through patch 6.50. For how the multitool upgrade layer works once you own any tool, see the [upgrades guide](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/).

## What Makes a Multitool "Royal"

Royal is a body type, not a class grade. Three properties define it:

1. **The look.** Ornate gold trim on a dark frame — no other tool in the pool looks remotely like it, which is the entire reason people hunt it.
2. **The statline.** Royals roll balanced, above-average stats across damage, mining, and scanner, with no specialty ceiling. Think of the profile as a [hauler](/starships/best-ships/) among tools: nothing weak, nothing record-setting.
3. **The rarity. Royals appear far less often than standard pistols and rifles and noticeably less often than experimentals. No farm guarantees one; patience does.**

Class grade — C, B, A, S — is a separate roll layered on top of the body type, exactly as with [S-class ships](/starships/s-class-ships/). An S-class royal is a genuinely excellent all-round tool; a C-class royal is a display piece.

## Where Royal Multitools Spawn

Royals share spawn rules with other stock multitools, just at much lower frequency:

| Spawn point | Notes |
|-------------|-------|
| Space station merchant cabinet | The main channel. The first tool offered at each station is drawn from that system's economy-tiered pool — wealthy systems for the best odds and grades |
| Minor settlements (planetside) | A second check per system. Same pool logic, different roll |
| Wealthy vs poor economies | Wealth and star rating shape the tool pool and its class odds — rich three-star economies are where every tool hunt converges |

What matters for the hunt is that the offered tool is generated when you look, influenced by the system — which is what makes rerolling possible at all.

## The S-Class Royal Hunt, Step by Step

1. **Stack wealth.** Use the galaxy map's economy filter to chart a route through wealthy three-star systems. This raises both the odds of a rare body type appearing and the odds of high class grades.
2. **Check the station first.** Dock, walk to the multitool cabinet, and inspect the offered tool — body type, class, slots, and price.
3. **Save before you look (on repeat visits).** If a station shows nothing, save your game before leaving; if you later want to reroll a promising system, reloading that save and re-checking redraws the offered tool.
4. **Check the planet, then move.** Hit the minor settlement before jumping on. One system gives you two draws from a good pool.
5. **Repeat until gold.** There is no counter or pity timer — frequency is purely probabilistic. Most hunters find a royal within a route of a dozen-plus wealthy systems; S-class takes longer.
6. **Buy with confidence.** You can own multiple multitools and switch freely, so buying a royal never locks you out of your combat or scanner tool. A trade-in discount applies when you swap an old tool in.

While you hop, the route doubles as everything else wealthy economies are good for — [exotic ship hunting](/starships/exotic-ship-locations/), freighter system checks, and station [nanite](/guides/nanites-farming/) runs. Treat the royal as the bonus objective of a rich-system loop, not a standalone grind.

## How Royal Stats Actually Compare

The honest comparison, for players deciding whether to buy:

| Category | Royal vs the field |
|----------|-------------------|
| Damage | Strong, balanced — below a good alien roll |
| Mining | Above average, well behind nothing |
| Scanner | Very good — below experimental's money-printing bonuses |
| Slots | Competitive technology and general slot counts |
| Looks | Unmatched. This is the actual selling point |

The practical read: a royal is the best *single* tool for a player who refuses to carry two tools and dislikes specializing. If you happily swap between a scanner build and a combat build — and the game lets you, freely — a royal is a luxury, not an upgrade.

## Building Out a Royal

Once owned, a royal takes the same upgrade plan as any tool: S-class upgrade modules from technology merchants, the three-module-per-family stacking limit, adjacency grouping around each core technology, and supercharged slots reserved for your damage or scanner chain. The full system is covered in [multitool upgrades](/multitools/multitool-upgrades/); weapon choices are covered in [weapon modes](/multitools/weapon-modes-guide/). A balanced tool rewards a balanced build — mining beam upgrades for the rock hours, boltcaster or neutron cannon modules for the pirate hours, scanner modules always.

## Is It Worth It?

For 2026 hunters: buy the first S-class or high-A royal you see if the price is pocket change to you, enjoy the flex, and keep your [experimental](/multitools/experimental-multitool/) for money days. Do not route your whole game around it — the dissonance content around [sentinel multitools](/multitools/sentinel-multitool/) gives a far more reliable reward loop per hour invested. The royal's value is that it is rare, handsome, and good enough. In a game about collecting beautiful machines, that is a complete sentence.
